## Strikes reported after truce announcement

Russian forces struck six Ukrainian energy facilities hours after US President Donald Trump announced an agreement on an energy ceasefire, according to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky. Trump stated on Sunday that the truce would be effective immediately, but Zelensky said Russia subsequently hit gas stations, energy infrastructure, and other facilities.

The state news agency Ukrinform reported blackouts in the city of Izyum after a strike destroyed power grids, while energy and railway infrastructure in western Ukraine was also targeted overnight. Ukraine's state-owned grid operator Ukrenergo reported new power cuts in Kharkiv, Donetsk, and Zaporizhzhia due to equipment damage, leading to electricity rationing in parts of the country.

Zelensky stated that 33 people had been injured in the strikes on energy facilities and described the attacks as an unwillingness by Russia to accept an energy ceasefire. Kyiv's mayor Vitali Klitschko reported that a drone hit a 25-story residential building in the Darnytskyi district on Monday evening, causing fires in the courtyard.

## International response and context

Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov stated on Monday that Ukraine does not deserve the respite a ceasefire would bring. Global conflict monitors and analysts have noted escalating attacks on energy infrastructure by both sides ahead of the winter season.

International reactions to the announcement varied. United Kingdom Prime Minister Andy Burnham expressed strong support for the sustained energy ceasefire call, alongside France, Canada, and the European Union. However, European Union foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas stated the bloc would welcome the ceasefire when it holds, noting that officials had not yet seen Russia stick to it. Estonia's foreign minister described the ceasefire as not working.

Trump's ceasefire announcement accompanied a plan to import Russian diesel into the United States and global markets, temporarily rolling back certain US energy sanctions. Zelensky previously described that move as a gift to Russian President Vladimir Putin while backing the proposed energy ceasefire as a potential step toward ending the conflict.
