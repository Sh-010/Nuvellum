## The tactical RPG renaissance

Recent releases such as *Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave* on the Nintendo Switch 2, alongside other titles like *Mario + Rabbids: Sparks of Hope*, *Final Fantasy Tactics: The Ivalice Chronicles*, and *Star Wars Zero Company*, have brought turn-based tactical role-playing games firmly back into the mainstream. This growing subgenre momentum highlights a broader industry trend toward strategic, grid-based combat and character-driven stories.

While Nintendo's *Fire Emblem* franchise—which gained massive mainstream traction with 2019's *Fire Emblem: Three Houses*—currently leads this resurgence, players and critics are recalling earlier genre staples. Among them is Sega's long-dormant *Shining Force* series, which originally debuted on the Sega Genesis and Mega Drive in the early 1990s.

## Origins of Shining Force

Released in North America and Europe in 1992 and 1993 respectively, *Shining Force* and *Shining Force 2* established themselves as pillars of early tactical console role-playing. Both titles are accessible via the Sega Genesis library included with a Nintendo Switch Online + Expansion Pack membership, while entries released for the Sega CD and Sega Saturn remain significantly harder to find legitimately.

Mechanically, the games share core similarities with modern tactical offerings. Players assemble and manage an army of diverse fighters divided into standard classes such as archers, mages, monks, knights, and warriors. Characters can also be promoted upon reaching specific levels using rare items, and rosters often grow large enough to require benching veteran units for new recruits.

## Distinctive roster and battle design

Where *Shining Force* distinguished itself was through its unconventional character designs. Beyond traditional fantasy archetypes, the series incorporated non-standard recruits including a werewolf, a robot, a flying jellyfish, a turtle, and a phoenix named Peter.

Battles take place on grid-based maps where players maneuver units before initiating separate pixel-art combat animations. Terrain features regularly impact movement and character safety, with objectives varying between defeating all enemies or vanquishing a specific leader across unconventional environments like floating sea rafts, circus tents, and maps modeled after Peru's Nazca lines.

Despite historical developer remarks from Hiroyuki Takahashi distancing the project from *Fire Emblem*'s tempo, the two franchises share strong structural similarities. Sega retains the rights to the *Shining* intellectual property, having previously shifted the series toward action-oriented tactical RPGs on the PlayStation Portable in the 2010s and the worldwide release of *Shining Resonance Refrain* in 2018. However, a modern return to the classic '90s tactical format remains unannounced.
