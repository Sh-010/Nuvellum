Several people have been killed or injured following a large fire near a Canadian military base in Alberta, according to the nation's military. 

The Canadian Armed Forces (CAF) stated that the full impact of the incident is currently being assessed. Authorities indicated that details regarding casualties and injuries will not be made public until that assessment is complete.

The fire broke out at around 16:00 local time on Saturday at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright, one of the country's busiest military training installations. 

## Cause and impact of the blaze

Citing unnamed military sources, Canadian TV network Global News reported that at least seven people were killed and dozens more injured when a gas line or gas well was struck during a live-fire training exercise.

The fire "spread rapidly due to high winds" and breached the fences of the base, according to the Municipal District of Wainwright. Regional gusts reached up to 100km/h (62mph), though local officials later reported that fire crews had contained the blaze.

Defence Minister David McGuinty described the situation as "very, very active and delicate," noting that he could not recall a similar incident in recent memory. Prime Minister Mark Carney stated that his thoughts were with all those affected.

## Emergency response and hospital strain

The Royal Alexandra Hospital in Edmonton declared a code orange, representing a disaster response protocol for high-casualty incidents. Dr Warren Thirsk, an emergency department physician at the hospital, told the Canadian Broadcasting Corporation (CBC) that the influx of patients risked overwhelming local healthcare facilities, describing it as a rare generational disaster.

Local authorities instructed residents to steer clear of the area and yield the right of way to emergency vehicles. Wainwright Mayor Bruce Pugh noted the deep ties between the town and the base, stating he believed the incident would become the region's largest-ever mass casualty event.

Gen Jennie Carignan, chief of Canada's defence staff, called it a "profoundly difficult moment" for the CAF. Meanwhile, neighbouring provinces mobilized support, with Saskatchewan Premier Scott Moe and British Columbia Premier David Eby offering resources and assistance to Alberta.

Canadian Forces Base Wainwright typically hosts about 690 military personnel and 300 civilian workers on a standard day, serving as the primary hub for Canadian military live-fire training.
