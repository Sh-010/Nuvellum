## Condemnation over prison footage

The Palestinian Commission of Detainees and Ex-Detainees Affairs condemned Israeli National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir after he published video footage on social media showing him confronting jailed Hamas figure Hassan Salama in his cell.

In the video, uploaded by Ben-Gvir on X, the far-right minister is seen telling Salama—who has been imprisoned for 30 years and is serving multiple life sentences for 1996 attacks—that he wanted to kill him and have him executed. Ben-Gvir stated that the recording was taken two weeks prior.

Raed Abu al-Hummus, head of the Palestinian Authority agency, described the footage as evidence that "degradation and depravity have reached their highest levels" and characterised conditions in Israeli prisons as "organised terrorism".

The Palestinian Prisoners Media Office labelled the publication "a cheap political and electoral stunt" ahead of Israel's October 27 elections, describing it as a "cowardly act of bullying against a defenceless and handcuffed prisoner" and holding Israeli authorities fully responsible for Salama's safety. Hamas stated that the threat "signals new crimes" against detainees and vowed to pursue efforts to secure their release.

## Pattern of actions and legislative context

Ben-Gvir's video follows previous incidents involving prison visits, including an August video showing him inspecting the cells of Palestinian women and boasting about inadequate conditions, as well as an instance last year where he taunted imprisoned Fatah leader Marwan Barghouti.

In March, Israel’s Knesset approved legislation allowing the death penalty for Palestinian prisoners in certain cases—a measure supported by Ben-Gvir and his Jewish Power party that does not apply to Jewish Israelis and has drawn regional criticism.

## Further policy statements

On Sunday, Ben-Gvir reiterated calls for the "emigration" of Palestinians from Gaza and the occupied West Bank. In July, he unveiled a plan titled "Disengagement 710" aimed at removing Gaza’s population, and stated an intention to establish a migration ministry if re-elected.

Ben-Gvir has also advocated for annexing portions of the occupied Palestinian territory and approved gun licenses for Israelis in additional settlements in the occupied territories, which are considered illegal under international law.
