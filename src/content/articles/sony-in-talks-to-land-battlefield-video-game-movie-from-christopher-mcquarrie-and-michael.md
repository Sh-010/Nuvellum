## Sony negotiates film acquisition

Sony is in talks to acquire a big-screen adaptation of the "Battlefield" video game franchise following a multi-studio bidding war. Sony declined to comment on the negotiations. 

Christopher McQuarrie is attached to write, direct, and produce the feature adaptation of the Electronic Arts military first-person shooter game. Michael B. Jordan is attached to produce through Outlier Society, alongside additional producers including Electronic Arts’ Jesse Stern, Emerald Neon’s Carter Swan, Vertigo’s Roy Lee, Miri Yoon of Vertigo, and Outlier Society president Elizabeth Raposo. Jordan could potentially star in the film, though that decision depends on several factors.

## Background on the franchise

Electronic Arts launched the "Battlefield" franchise in 2002 with "Battlefield 1942," which was set during World War Two. The games center on large-scale, online multiplayer battles between opposing military forces, spanning historical events, alternate history, and future conflicts over two decades. The series' 18th installment, "Battlefield 6," was released the previous year and is set between 2027 and 2028, following a conflict between a fractured NATO and a private military company.

## Upcoming projects for the creators

For McQuarrie, the project follows his work on the "Mission: Impossible" franchise. He is also set to direct Arnold Schwarzenegger in "King Conan" and work with Doug Liman on an outer-space film starring Tom Cruise.

Jordan has prior experience with video game and action intellectual property, having starred in Amazon's "Without Remorse" as John Clark and developed a "Rainbow Six" adaptation. His upcoming roles include starring in, directing, and producing a reimagining of "The Thomas Crown Affair" for Amazon MGM Studios, as well as starring in Universal's "Miami Vice '85" as Ricardo "Rico" Tubbs alongside Austin Butler.
