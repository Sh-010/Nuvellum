## Best Film Award

Sergei Loznitsa’s “Imperium,” which revisits the Soviet Union of the early 1970s through footage shot by Italian cameramen, won the best film award at the 70th BFI London Film Festival competition. The prizes were announced as the festival drew to its close.

The competition jury was led by filmmaker Sam Pollard and included filmmaker Mariano Llinás and DDA chief strategy officer Julie La Bassiere. Describing the winning entry, the jury stated that the film is richly evocative and emotionally engaging, offering a visually arresting perspective of life before the Soviet Union broke apart.

## First Feature and Documentary Winners

In the first feature competition, the Sutherland Award went to Sompot Chidgasornpongse’s fiction feature debut, Thailand’s Oscar submission “9 Temples to Heaven.” The jury for this category was presided over by actor Amir El-Masry, alongside Alemberg Ang and Rosa Bosch. Bertil Nilsson’s “Masc” received a special mention in the same section, recognized as a fresh British talent with an unusual concept and script.

The Grierson Award for documentary was presented to Asmahan Bkerat’s feature directorial debut, “Concrete Land.” The film follows a mixed-race family in Jordan resisting displacement and hostility. Sandhya Suri headed the documentary jury alongside Kate Fitzpatrick and Andee Ryder. Theo Rollason’s “Queer Edward II,” assembled from archive material, earned a special mention in the documentary category.

## Short Film Prize

Rahul Roye’s “Aman” won the Short Film Award. The film centers on a trans man who learns he is pregnant the night before his gender-affirming surgery. The shorts jury, led by writer-producer Helen Simmons alongside Leo Reich and Jack Casey, praised its painterly cinematography and assured storytelling. Elizabeth Hobbs’ “Daughters of the Late Colonel” received a special mention for its visual style. Audience voting for the festival's remaining feature and British discovery prizes was scheduled to close on October 21.
