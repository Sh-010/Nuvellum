## Complaint to the Organisation of American States

Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva’s party plans to file a complaint against Meta with the Organisation of American States (OAS), alleging election interference, his campaign announced Wednesday. The leftist leader is seeking a fourth nonconsecutive term in an October 25 presidential runoff against Senator Flavio Bolsonaro, the son of far-right former president Jair Bolsonaro.

The party's communications secretary, Eden Valadares, told reporters that the move was ordered by Lula himself. "It's time to denounce (Meta) before the international community, starting with the OAS," Valadares said.

## Allegations of online restrictions

The campaign maintains that Meta is harming Lula's electoral bid by restricting his online content. Specifically, the campaign claimed that Meta recently prevented the president's profile from promoting Instagram feed posts and that an official account post experienced an interruption in view counts that affected its reach.

Additionally, the campaign stated that prior to the first round of voting on October 9—in which Flavio Bolsonaro finished first—the profiles of Lula and other leftist leaders "suddenly disappeared from Instagram's search function."

Meta’s Brazil office has denied the allegations, calling them "simply false" and "not supported by the facts."

## Campaign response and broader claims

In response to the alleged interference, Lula's campaign scheduled a Thursday meeting with international election observers. 

Lula has separately claimed that the United States is attempting to influence the elections in favor of Flavio Bolsonaro, whose father is an ally of US President Donald Trump. Following the first round of results, Lula launched a renewed social media push featuring video addresses to his online followers.
