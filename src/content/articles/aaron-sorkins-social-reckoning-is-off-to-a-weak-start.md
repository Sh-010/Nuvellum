## Domestic box office projections

“The Social Reckoning,” Aaron Sorkin’s follow-up film to “The Social Network,” is on track to earn $5.5 million at the domestic box office over its opening weekend, according to Sunday estimates. This figure falls short of the studio's projected $7 million to $8 million range, indicating that the new release is unlikely to match the box office success of the 2010 film.

By comparison, “The Social Network” opened with $22 million and grossed a total of $97 million domestically. That earlier film also earned an Oscar for Sorkin and became recognized as an influential depiction of Silicon Valley and startup culture.

## Shift in focus and reception

While “The Social Network” focused on the early days of Facebook, the sequel centers on whistleblower Frances Haugen’s decision to leak the documents known as the Facebook files. The new movie explores Senate hearings and algorithm changes, arriving during a changing theatrical landscape over the past 16 years. 

In addition to the box office figures, the film's reviews have been described as underwhelming. Other releases exploring real-world tech figures have also debuted, including Alex Gibney’s documentary “Musk,” which earned $69,000 across four theaters during its opening weekend.
