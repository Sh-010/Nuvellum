US President Donald Trump stated he may ask European countries to release strategic diesel reserves as he weighs a potential ban on American fuel exports. The proposed restrictions aim to lower prices for US drivers, truckers, and businesses ahead of midterm elections in November. 

Global diesel markets have faced severe pressure due to the ongoing conflict involving Iran and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, which transports a significant portion of the world's oil and gas. Additional constraints stem from sanctions on Russia following its invasion of Ukraine, subsequent Ukrainian attacks on Russian refining capacity, and a reported halt on October fuel product exports by Chinese refiners.

## Impact on the UK and Europe

The UK relies on imports for over half of its diesel, with 31% originating from the US and substantial volumes also supplied by the Netherlands and Belgium. UK motoring organisation the RAC reported that average pump prices reached record highs near 200p per litre.

UK Energy Minister Martin McCluskey engaged in discussions with European counterparts, including the European Union, regarding the supply threats and potential coordinated responses. A UK government source indicated familiarity with ongoing talks and noted that European countries retain emergency reserves from previous coordinated releases. A government spokesperson stated that the UK maintains a diverse and resilient supply, while stressing that there is no cause for concern about potential shortages.

## Global market risks

The US exports between 1.2 and 1.5 million barrels of diesel per day, primarily serving markets in Latin America alongside European importers such as France, the Netherlands, and the UK.

While a US export ban could flood the domestic American market and reduce prices locally, energy experts warn it would likely cause international prices to skyrocket unless alternative supplies are secured. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent urged Europe via social media to prepare for an immediate release of emergency supplies so that US workers do not bear the burden of soaring costs.

Under International Energy Agency regulations, member states including the UK must maintain oil stocks equivalent to 90 days of net imports, while EU rules mandate reserves covering 61 days of domestic consumption. A European Commission spokesperson confirmed that high-level contacts and discussions with the US administration are underway regarding the diesel situation.
