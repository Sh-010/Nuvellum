## Nationwide ground stop halted departures

A nationwide ground stop halted airplane departures across the United States following communication problems at regional air traffic control centers. Officials stated that the disruption stemmed from outdated equipment and the integration of new technology rather than any cyberattack. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin reported that introducing new tools into an aging air system overloaded it, though investigators found no footprints of a malicious act.

The Federal Aviation Administration lifted the order allowing departures to resume, but airlines faced extended recovery times as numerous aircraft and crews remained displaced. Hundreds of flights experienced diversions to alternative airports, impacting travel schedules for thousands of passengers nationwide.

## Impact on travelers and transport sectors

Passengers across major hubs encountered sudden delays and uncertainty as airport screens reflected widespread schedule changes. The disruption also affected cargo operations and left several professional and college sports teams stranded while attempting to travel for weekend games.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed the travel chaos on old hardware, while the White House continues to lobby Congress for billions in additional funding to modernize the nation's air traffic control infrastructure. Investigators are reviewing whether the incident connected to a newly implemented artificial intelligence system designed to assist with aircraft rerouting.
