Long before unauthorized clips of Grand Theft Auto 6 surfaced online, Grand Theft Auto 5 made video game leak history ahead of its launch. 

## The PlayStation Store snafu and early file access

The leaks began a few weeks prior to GTA 5's official release after a PlayStation Store issue allowed players who had pre-ordered the game to access some of its files. Initially, the files appeared relatively minor, revealing how much storage space the game would require on PS3 hard drives and listing specific soundtrack songs. Additional leaks stemming from early strategy guides also exposed the game's map ahead of schedule.

Rockstar expressed frustration over the situation, stating in a blog post that the team was "deeply disappointed by leaks and spoilers being spread in advance of the game's launch." Sony subsequently pulled the preload option and issued apologies to fans and Rockstar for the error.

## Escalating leaks and piracy

The situation intensified rapidly as players began sharing details regarding the game's story and ending. Both Rockstar and Sony issued warnings that players risked bans for spreading spoilers early. The situation escalated further when the finished game was uploaded to torrent websites, enabling individuals with jailbroken consoles to play the title early without paying.

At the time, digital games had only recently entered the mainstream, making leaks of that magnitude uncommon. Community reactions captured the era's technology limitations, with low-resolution 240p videos and content shared on platforms like Vine. Despite the grainy footage and complaints about how the leakers played the game, the 2013 incident remains a notable precedent in pre-release video game data breaches.
