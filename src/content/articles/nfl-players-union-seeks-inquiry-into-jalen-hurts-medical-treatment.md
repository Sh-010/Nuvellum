## Union demands review

The National Football League Players Association has called for an investigation into how the Philadelphia Eagles managed quarterback Jalen Hurts' blood clot condition. The union's demand follows the league's public defense of the team's medical staff.

Reports indicated that the Eagles would face no disciplinary action regarding the matter. The controversy arose after media reports stated the quarterback stayed in a hospital until the early morning hours and received no sleep prior to Philadelphia's game against the Jacksonville Jaguars in London. Further tests eventually ruled him out for the remainder of the season after a diagnosis of deep vein thrombosis and bilateral pulmonary emboli.

## League defends medical staff

Dr. Allen Sills, the NFL's chief medical officer, expressed total trust in the team's medical personnel during the league's annual fall meeting. Sills characterized the approach as patient-focused and conservative, stating that disease progression can occur despite proper medical actions. 

NFL executive vice president Jeff Miller also disputed reports regarding sleep deprivation, estimating that the player slept for several hours prior to heading to the stadium. While the league stated that an inquiry was not initially planned, the players association emphasized that establishing facts is vital to guarantee athlete protection.

## Diagnosis and recovery

Hurts initially reported a left calf injury to medical staff after practice in London. Specialists diagnosed a superficial vein thrombosis before he flew back and continued training. 

Dr. Caron Rockman, a vascular surgeon not involved in the case, noted that managing such conditions requires individual judgment and that superficial vein thrombosis does not necessarily disqualify a player from flying. Hurts' ongoing recovery requires treatment with blood thinners, keeping him sidelined from all football activities until the medication regimen concludes.
