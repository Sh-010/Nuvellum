Violent clashes, looting, and fires broke out across France on Saturday during demonstrations against the cost of living and hardship in schools. The French government reported that at least 1,200 people were arrested as an estimated 550,000 demonstrators took to the streets.

## Clashes and arson in Paris and regional cities

In Paris, the main afternoon march appeared largely peaceful, but disorder escalated into the evening. Riot police deployed tear gas and water cannon against crowds as groups lit fires, torched vehicles in areas including the Place de la République, and ransacked shops. A restaurant was gutted by fire, and the French interior ministry reported targeted acts of violence by hooded individuals in the capital as well as Marseille, Rennes, Toulouse, and Angers.

Fires also caused severe damage to civic buildings outside Paris. In Limoges, a large fire at the town hall caused the roof to partly collapse. In Lyon, Mayor Grégory Doucet described the burning of the city's tourist office as a tragedy after local police reported that hostile individuals prevented firefighters from tackling the blaze. In Saint-Étienne, Mayor Régis Juanico stated that a young man had his hand blown off by a small explosive.

Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu stated that 150 police officers were injured in the unrest, describing the violence as having an "extreme intensity."

## Political reactions and background

The widespread disorder comes amid political uncertainty as France heads toward next year's presidential election following the departure of President Emmanuel Macron. Hard-left La France Insoumise figure Jean-Luc Mélenchon accused police of unleashing on protesters while condemning looting and arson. Prime Minister Lecornu called Mélenchon's comments dangerous and cynical, alleging that some groups were using the protests as a warm-up for the presidential election.

National Rally leader Marine Le Pen asserted that legitimate demands voiced by demonstrators had been held hostage by the far left and accused Mélenchon of fuelling the violence. President Macron stated that individuals who attacked prefectures, businesses, and town halls would be prosecuted and punished.

The demonstrations follow weeks of student protests over staff shortages and dilapidated facilities in secondary schools, which have been joined by trade union members and members of the gilets jaunes protest movement.
