Two prominent creators of futuristic interior decor, whose bold works appeared in famous cinematic space adventures, died within weeks of each other. Their passings were confirmed following reports by the New York Times.

## Steen Ostergaard

Danish designer Steen Ostergaard passed away on October 13 at the age of 90 in a nursing home located in Harlev, a suburb of Aarhus in eastern Denmark. Known for utilizing plastic to create modernistic pop art styles during the 1960s, his creations extended into both television and film, including James Bond productions and NASA's Johnson Space Center in Houston.

Ostergaard is particularly recognized for his Model 265 President lounge chair. In *Star Trek II: The Wrath of Khan* (1982), the sloping, black-and-white plastic seat served as a 20th-century antique owned by Admiral James T. Kirk in the 23rd century, and also appeared in the original television series. Additionally, he created the 1967 A-Line Model 290 stackable plastic chair, recognized for its melting, twisted aesthetic.

## Olivier Mourgue

French designer Olivier Mourgue died on October 5 at his home in Plouguiel, France, at the age of 87. Director Stanley Kubrick personally recruited Mourgue for the 1968 film *2001: A Space Odyssey* after discovering his work in a British publication.

Mourgue's serpentine, bright red "Djinn" chairs were featured prominently within the white interior of the space station Hilton hotel in Kubrick's film. Prior to that appearance, a pink version of the Djinn lounge was utilized in the 1967 James Bond parody *Casino Royale*.
