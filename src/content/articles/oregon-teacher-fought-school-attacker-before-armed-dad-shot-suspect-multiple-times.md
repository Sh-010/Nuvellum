## Teacher and bystander intervene during attack

An Oregon teacher threw her mobile phone before tackling and attempting to disarm a teenager who opened fire on a special educational needs class at Silver Rail Elementary School in Bend. As she wrestled a shotgun from the suspect on the classroom floor, an armed bystander ran inside and killed the teenager.

Police stated that the 16-year-old suspect, identified as Griffin J John II, had previously been a student at the school. Armed with a shotgun and approximately 44 additional rounds of ammunition, the suspect fired as many as 15 rounds inside the classroom during the Thursday lunchtime incident.

Bend Police Chief Mike Krantz said both the teacher and the father who intervened saved lives and prevented further bloodshed. The teacher was uninjured in the struggle.

## Victims and casualties

Four people were killed in the attack: three children and a teacher. Police identified two of the victims on Friday as five-year-old Rhoam Summer and 25-year-old teacher Meghan Lambert. 

The families of the other two child victims—an eight-year-old and an 11-year-old—did not wish to have their names publicly released. Two other individuals were injured, including a 54-year-old teacher still receiving hospital treatment and a seven-year-old student in serious but stable condition.

## Bystander identification and response

While officials did not officially confirm the name of the father who intervened, family members identified him to United States media as Kaylub Giamanco, a 36-year-old carpenter. According to his brother, Giamanco was driving past the school when he witnessed the suspect shooting through a window to enter.

Police stated the bystander carried a legally concealed .40 calibre weapon in his vehicle, retrieved the pistol, and entered the building, firing multiple times at the suspect. 

Giamanco's mother, Kenda Freeman, stated her son called her shortly after the incident in a traumatised state. United States President Donald Trump praised the bystander as a hero, while Oregon Governor Tina Kotek noted that the actions stopped further loss of life while carrying a heavy emotional burden. Police continue to investigate the suspect's movements and motive.
