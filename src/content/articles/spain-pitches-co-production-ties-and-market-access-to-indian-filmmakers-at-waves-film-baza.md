Spain used its position as Country of Honor at the International Film Festival of India to advocate for closer cinematic partnerships with Indian producers. During a panel at the WAVES Film Bazaar in Goa, representatives from Spanish cultural and trade agencies outlined financial incentives, technical infrastructure, and access to broader markets.

## Strengthening bilateral ties and incentives
Camilo Vázquez of Spain's Institute of Cinematography and Audiovisual Arts described co-production as a vital mechanism for improving financing and fostering artistic exchange. Lara Molina of the Spain Film Commission detailed tax incentives available to international productions, while actor and producer Usha Jadhav noted practical benefits such as skilled crews and eight-hour working shifts.

Miguel Fabián Salazar, representing ICEX, pointed out complementary strengths between the two nations, citing India's production scale and animation capabilities alongside Spain's experienced crews and European access. Guillermo Rodríguez of Casa de la India noted that the 2012 audio-visual co-production agreement between the two countries is currently being updated with clearer procedural guidelines.

## Connecting cultural superpowers
Rodríguez emphasized the broader market potential of combining the industries. He noted that partnerships could grant Indian creators access to a Spanish-speaking consumer base exceeding 500 million people.

The session concluded with a presentation of five Spanish projects seeking Indian collaborators, including the romance "Serendipia," the Andalusian feature debut "My Parents Grand Bazaar," the animated project "Mumu," the documentary "Aana and Aai – The War to Become an Artist," and "The Dragon Letter" by director Stanzin Jigmet.
