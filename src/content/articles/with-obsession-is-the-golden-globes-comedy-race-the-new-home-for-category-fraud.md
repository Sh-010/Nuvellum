## The debate over genre classification in awards races

Category manipulation has long appeared in film and television awards cycles, with studios submitting projects and performances in categories perceived to offer better odds of winning a trophy. In the current awards circuit, however, industry scrutiny surrounding these decisions has intensified.

Recent discussions have focused on comedy categories at the Golden Globes. Focus Features submitted Curry Barker’s thriller-leaning film *Obsession*—which was made for $750,000 and surpassed $500 million globally—as a musical or comedy. The submission placed breakout star Inde Navarrette into the comedy actress race. While the film features comedic moments, industry observers note that audiences primarily witness a woman losing control of her mind and body.

Following that submission, Bleecker Street entered Mike Leigh’s *Tender Loving Care* into the same comedy categories. That film includes hospital visits and aging parents dealing with life's final chapters alongside lighter moments like drunken sing-alongs.

## Historical precedent and current rules

This phenomenon is not new to the Golden Globes. Past examples include Ridley Scott’s *The Martian*, a survival drama about a man stranded on Mars, winning the motion picture comedy category, and Universal's entry of the horror film *Get Out* as a comedy in 2017. In contrast, Focus submitted *Promising Young Woman* as a comedy in 2020, but voters reassigned it to the drama category.

Under the current Golden Globes rulebook, a movie "primarily intended to have a serious or dramatic tone" featuring "comedic overtones or elements" belongs in the drama category. The organization evaluates entries using its "sole discretion" through a review committee without an appeals process when disputes arise regarding genre classification or lead-versus-supporting campaigns.

Some awards strategists attempt to preempt potential committee objections by supplying documentation such as filmmaker quotes and source materials. However, industry insiders note that attempts to maneuver around genre definitions do not guarantee success if the underlying material clearly points to a different classification.
