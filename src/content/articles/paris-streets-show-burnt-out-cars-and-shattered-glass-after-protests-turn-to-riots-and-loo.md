France awoke to widespread damage on Sunday following a day of riots and demonstrations that left shopfronts shattered, burnt-out cars, charred bikes, and debris on the pavements of Paris.

Police arrested about 1,800 people on Saturday as approximately 550,000 demonstrators took to the streets for protests against rising fuel prices, the cost of living, and deteriorating public services, including schools. While the demonstrations were largely peaceful, groups of rioters—some dressed in black and wearing masks—also emerged in Paris, Marseille, and other towns, setting fire to cars and buildings.

## Clashes and widespread destruction

In Paris, protesters clashed with riot police who deployed tear gas and water cannon. Groups lit street fires, torched vehicles, and gutted at least one restaurant. Further south in Marseille, protesters used a metal fence as a battering ram to break into the city hall. In Lille, a McDonald's was set on fire and an Apple store was broken into and looted, while designer store frontages including Ralph Lauren, Gucci, and Versace were smashed in Paris.

France's interior ministry reported that more than 500 police officers, gendarmes, and firefighters were injured during the unrest. According to the Retail and Trade Federation, around 1,500 shops and retailers were damaged.

In Lyon, Mayor Grégory Doucet described the burning of the city's tourist office as a tragedy, noting that hostile individuals prevented firefighters from safely tackling the blaze. Fire crews also battled a fire at Limoges town hall that caused part of the building to collapse. Maurice Barate, the prefect for the Haute-Vienne department, stated that a "wild horde" had "deliberately" set fire to the 143-year-old building, leaving it largely lost. In Saint-Étienne, Mayor Régis Juanico reported that a young man had his hand blown off by a small explosive.

## Official responses

France's Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez stated that while demonstrating is a fundamental right, "outrages" were committed by "several thousand far-left individuals" who targeted public buildings and confronted police, later joined by delinquents who looted and ransacked businesses.

Student protests have drawn hundreds of thousands to the streets since beginning late last month, with participants demanding additional investment in schools, criticizing overcrowded classrooms, run-down buildings, and a lack of teachers and staff.
