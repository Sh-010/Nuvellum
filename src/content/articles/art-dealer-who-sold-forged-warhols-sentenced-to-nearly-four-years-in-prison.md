## Prison sentence and restitution

A Miami art dealer who admitted to selling forged Andy Warhol artwork has been sentenced to nearly three years and 10 months in federal prison. Leslie Roberts, 62, was sentenced by US District Judge Roy Altman on 2 October. According to the Associated Press, the judge also ordered Roberts to repay $2.6m stolen through the scheme involving artwork falsely attributed to the pop art movement leader.

According to the US Department of Justice, Roberts created fraudulent invoices, authentication documents, and counterfeit stamps to deceive buyers. He sold the fakes through his Miami art gallery, falsely claiming he acquired the pieces from the Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts and providing fake invoices that appeared to originate from the organization.

## Co-conspirators and legal proceedings

Roberts pleaded guilty in June to conspiring to commit wire fraud. As part of the scheme, he enlisted Carlos Rodriguez Melendez to pose as an employee of a New York-based auction company to falsely authenticate the artwork. Melendez also pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit wire fraud.

Jason Reding Quiñones, the US attorney for the southern district of Florida, stated that prosecuting Roberts was necessary because art fraud undermines confidence in the legitimate art market and victimizes buyers relying on authenticity. Reding Quiñones noted that Roberts exploited the trust collectors place in art dealers, adding that the sentence sends a clear message that those who profit through deception will be held accountable.

## Wider art counterfeiting cases

Other individuals have faced convictions in recent months related to the sale of fake Warhol works. In April, a father and daughter from New Jersey, Erwin Bankowski and Karolina Bankowska, admitted to commissioning an artist in Poland to produce at least 200 Warhol, Banksy, and Pablo Picasso fakes, defrauding buyers of at least $2m. The US Department of Justice stated that the pair pleaded guilty to running a counterfeiting scheme that tricked galleries and auction houses, and they could face up to 20 years’ imprisonment alongside at least $1.9m in restitution when sentenced.
