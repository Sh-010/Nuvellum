## The rise of Instinct

Before major tech companies released their own AI products, the startup Instinct launched its artificial intelligence agent in August with an unusual approach: invite-only access, no marketing, and a minimal web presence. Despite the quiet rollout, the service quickly gained attention for its straightforward interface via text message and its capability to handle daily chores such as booking appointments and sending follow-up emails.

Founded by Noah Shinn, an early employee of Sierra, Instinct secured a $10 billion valuation in late September following a $1 billion Series C funding round. The assistant operates without a traditional app or a monthly subscription fee, requiring either an invitation from an existing user or approval from a waitlist.

## Functionality and competition

Instinct functions primarily through standard text channels including iMessage, WhatsApp, and email, while integrating with personal services such as Google Workspace, Slack, and Notion via a virtual machine. Founder Noah Shinn emphasizes that the product is built strictly as a personal assistant for everyday life, contrasting it with platforms that serve a broader range of customer needs.

However, the competitive landscape shifted following the arrival of Muse and Dots—similar products introduced by larger technology companies including Meta and OpenAI. While these competitors incorporate distinct branding elements like animal mascots and anthropomorphized designs aimed at mainstream consumers, Instinct maintains a text-first interface.

Testing demonstrates that Instinct can manage multi-step online tasks, coordinate schedules, and execute form-filling requests. At the same time, testers note limitations common to current AI agents, such as occasional data oversights when navigating complex websites designed for human users.

## Business model and future monetization

While Instinct remains free for invite-only users, its long-term financial structure remains under development. Industry analysts suggest that venture-backed AI startups prioritize rapid user growth to establish market value, with potential revenue streams eventually coming from customer fees, corporate acquisition, or indirect monetization such as targeted product recommendations and advertising. Analysts note that as consumer-facing agents become more commerce-heavy, advertising may ultimately play a role in sustaining free tiers across the sector.
