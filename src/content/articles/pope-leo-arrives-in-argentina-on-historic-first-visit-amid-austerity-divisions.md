Pope Leo XIV arrived in Buenos Aires on Sunday from Uruguay, marking the first papal visit to the late Pope Francis's homeland in nearly four decades. The pontiff's arrival comes as Argentina remains deeply split following three years of austerity measures introduced by libertarian President Javier Milei.

## Papal Welcome and Public Expectations

President Milei greeted the head of the Catholic Church at the foot of his plane. Crowds gathered near the presidential palace and along transit routes, hoping the visit would help ease political tensions. Some citizens expressed desires for national unity, while others voiced strong opposition to the administration's policies. 

During his four-day schedule, Leo is expected to advocate for society's vulnerable populations. His itinerary includes visits to a home for disabled individuals and a prison, alongside three open-air masses. Church organizers anticipate millions of faithful will participate in the scheduled events.

## Economic Tensions and Church Relations

President Milei took office with an agenda aimed at reviving the economy and controlling inflation by scaling back the welfare state, affecting public hospitals, universities, and pensioners. While the drastic measures have reduced inflation and maintained a loyal base of supporters, they have also drawn heavy criticism. The local Catholic Church has rebuked the administration for evaluating essential social services purely as budget constraints, and critics have raised concerns over restrictions on democratic freedoms and the right to protest.

Leo's visit to Argentina follows a stop in Uruguay, where he addressed drug violence and child poverty. The Argentine leg also serves to engage a shifting religious landscape, as recent polling indicates a decline in self-identified Catholics compared to previous decades.
