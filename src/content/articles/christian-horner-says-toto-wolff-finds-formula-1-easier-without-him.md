Former Red Bull team principal Christian Horner believes Mercedes counterpart Toto Wolff is finding life in Formula 1 "easier" following Horner's departure from the pit-wall in July 2025.

Horner and Wolff shared a fierce rivalry over the past decade, highlighted by the 2021 season where Max Verstappen halted Mercedes' streak of seven consecutive drivers' titles. Red Bull subsequently secured the constructors' championship in 2022 as Mercedes entered a challenging phase, prompting numerous verbal exchanges between the two team bosses.

## Rivalry and Mercedes' dominance

Ahead of the release of his book *Drive*, Horner reflected on their history with Sky Sports News. "Sport needs rivalry. Toto and I had some great jousting over the years," Horner said. "There was never any malice behind it. It was all sporting and he's got it a bit easier at the moment, so he's probably finding life a bit easier without me there."

Mercedes is positioned to reclaim both the drivers' and constructors' titles this season, with Kimi Antonelli poised to secure the drivers' championship as early as the United States Grand Prix in Austin.

## Addressing Ferrari speculation and future plans

Over the past year, Horner has been linked to several teams, including Ferrari, Alpine, and Aston Martin. Reports previously suggested he could replace Frederic Vasseur at Ferrari, though Horner stated he felt sympathy for Vasseur regarding the intense media scrutiny generated by an earlier interview.

Regarding his own future, Horner stated he is in no rush to return and is currently enjoying time away from the demanding Formula 1 travel schedule after completing 404 consecutive races.

"If the right opportunity in a team existed, I'd certainly consider it seriously," Horner said. "Right here, right now, I don't see that at Alpine. All of those teams should be striving to win... If the opportunity opens up, I'll certainly look at it very strongly. But if I don't, then I'm comfortable with that."
