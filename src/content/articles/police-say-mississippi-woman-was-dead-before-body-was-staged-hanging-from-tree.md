Police in Mississippi stated in court that they believe a 29-year-old black woman was already dead before her body was "staged" and placed hanging from a tree. The disclosure was made during a Friday court hearing by a detective investigating the death of Tasia Fortune, a mother of four.

Fortune's body was discovered in August hanging from a tree at an abandoned home in Jackson, Mississippi. The location and nature of the discovery generated significant concern within the majority-black city, evoking the state's historical record of racial violence and lynchings. Following weeks of speculation and public demands for information, law enforcement officials announced multiple arrests in the case.

## Investigation and Arrests

A police news release noted that a medical examiner determined Fortune's death was a homicide and that the scene and hanging were staged. Authorities have not publicly disclosed the exact cause of death or the specific details regarding why they believe the hanging was staged.

During Friday's proceedings, the detective testified that investigators believe Fortune was killed in a dispute connected to drugs. Christy Spivey, Fortune's mother, told reporters outside the Jackson Police Department that she was previously informed by the coroner that officials were not treating the incident as an actual hanging and that her daughter was deceased prior to being placed in the tree. Spivey also stated that police indicated the matter was drug-related.

Three individuals have been arrested in connection with the investigation. One suspect, 51-year-old Jarques Ratliff, has pleaded not guilty and remains in custody without bond. A second suspect, Earnest Lloyd Jr., 25, was arrested during the week. A third individual, 45-year-old Eric Clark, turned himself in to authorities on Friday, was charged with murder, and is scheduled to make a court appearance on Monday. All three suspects are black.

## Broader Context and Congressional Inquiries

Fortune's death forms part of a broader pattern of recent cases that have prompted federal lawmakers to press for increased oversight. In September, US Representative Ayanna Pressley of Massachusetts led a letter to Congress containing signatures from more than 50 lawmakers. The letter called on the United States Department of Justice and the FBI to investigate all hanging deaths of black people nationwide, citing at least 10 documented incidents.

Reflecting on the ongoing proceedings and the loss of her daughter—who leaves behind four young children aged 7, 5, 4, and 1—Spivey wore a shirt reading "Justice for Tasia Fortune" while speaking to the media. She emphasized her desire for a thorough and accurate judicial process, stating, "I just hope they have the right people."
