Workers across the United Kingdom's film, television, music, and theater sectors can now report whistleblowing concerns—including abuse, harassment, and discrimination—through a new portal launched by the Creative Industries Independent Standards Authority (CIISA).

## Official recognition and early reports

Operating as an officially designated whistleblowing body following recognition in Parliament, CIISA allows individuals to submit confidential reports regarding experiences that relate to CIISA Standards and may have a wider impact on others. The organization stated it will take careful steps to protect the identities of those who come forward.

Following a soft-launch in June, CIISA received approximately 30 reports as of October 1 and anticipates an increase in volume. Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy stated that safety at work is non-negotiable and that the government expects employers across the creative industries to cooperate with CIISA.

## Industry power imbalances and legal protections

Philippa Childs, head of the entertainment workers union Bectu, attributed the creation of CIISA to extreme power imbalances, freelance employment structures, and short-term project cycles that she said allowed sexual predators and bullies to hide within the creative industries. 

CIISA chair Helena Kennedy KC noted that protecting the people in the sector is central to the organization's ambitions, while emphasizing that as an industry-funded body, CIISA requires the full backing of the sector.

Workers reporting concerns to CIISA may qualify for additional legal protections under U.K. whistleblowing law, including protections related to previously signed nondisclosure agreements. However, CIISA's initial reporting service focuses on mapping out sector-wide behaviors and practices rather than immediately acting on individual cases.
