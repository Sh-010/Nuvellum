## Trump announces Russian diesel imports

President Donald Trump has announced a deal to import Russian diesel into the United States as part of efforts to address high fuel prices. According to Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin agreed to release 300,000 tonnes of diesel immediately to the American and global marketplace, with subsequent shipments of 500,000 tonnes in November and one million tonnes thereafter.

The US Treasury is suspending sanctions on Russian diesel exports until 7 April as part of the agreement, while other assets such as those in American banks remain frozen. The US Treasury formally issued a temporary licence allowing Russian diesel into the market following the announcement. Trump stated that an additional three million tonnes could be delivered within a short period, depending on the condition of Russian refineries affected by Ukrainian attacks.

Putin stated that he had discussed the global energy situation with Trump and confirmed Russia's willingness to supply oil and petroleum products to the US and global markets. Kirill Dmitriev, an envoy for Putin, welcomed the arrangement on social media, stating that energy cooperation between the two countries would benefit the world.

## Criticism from Ukraine and questions from analysts

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky strongly condemned the announcement, describing it as a gift to Putin and an investment in the ongoing war. Zelensky stated that gifts to Putin would not work for peace and warned that Russia would respond with further terror, while urging the United States to maintain strong support for Ukraine.

Zelensky also defended Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian oil refineries, which have contributed to an estimated 30% drop in Russian diesel production according to the International Energy Agency. Trump has previously expressed displeasure over those strikes, blaming them for rising fuel prices, whereas Zelensky maintained they are a response to attacks on Ukraine's energy infrastructure.

Industry analysts have questioned whether Russia possesses the capacity to provide the stated quantities of diesel and whether the agreement will meaningfully reduce pump prices for American consumers. Tim Armitage, an investment strategist at Quilter Cheviot, noted that the initial release equated to approximately 2.25 million barrels compared to daily US consumption of around 3.8 million barrels, suggesting it would not make a material difference to pump prices.

A UK government spokesperson said its position remained unchanged, stating that the UK will continue working with international partners to support Ukraine, maintain the toughest sanctions regime against Russia, and secure a lasting peace.

## Impact of the Iran conflict and domestic pressure

The agreement follows months of rising fuel costs driven by the conflict with Iran that began in February, which pushed gasoline and diesel prices higher in the United States and contributed to broader inflation. The average diesel price in the US is currently $6.28 a gallon, down from a record high of $6.53 at the end of September.

With US midterm congressional elections approaching, Trump has faced political pressure regarding fuel costs. Earlier measures to lower prices have included urging G7 nations to release 100 million barrels of oil and diesel from stockpiles, considering a suspension of the federal tax on gasoline, and permitting red dye diesel to be used on US highways without federal tax. The deal with Russia also marks a shift following recent US congressional legislation authorizing new sanctions and tariffs on nations importing Russian oil and gas.
