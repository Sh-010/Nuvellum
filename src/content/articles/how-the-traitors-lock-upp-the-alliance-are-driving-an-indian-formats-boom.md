India's unscripted television sector is experiencing significant growth, transitioning from a passive importer of formats to setting trends on an international stage. Industry analysts attribute the shift to streaming expansion, increased production budgets, and a focus on scale and future advertising rollouts.

## Rise of streaming and captive reality

Major platforms are building for scale, pulling programming toward repeatable, mass-audience formats. This shift has manifested through original formats from the local production community, major refreshes of international hits, and streaming executives willing to take risks on new concepts.

JioHotstar introduced celebrity reality series *Bhojpuri Bawaal* and launched six simultaneous language versions of *Bigg Boss* in September. Netflix has driven cultural conversations with captive reality series *Lock Upp* and *Desi Bling*. Meanwhile, Prime Video introduced *Alliance*, marking the first international remake of Talpa Studios' competition format.

Monika Shergill, vice president of content for Netflix India, noted that the market is seeing an influx of young voices and expanding talent pools catering to the country's diverse population.

## Breakout hits and international interest

Produced by Balaji Telefilms, Netflix's *Lock Upp* pits celebrities against each other in a jail-themed setting. After launching on other platforms in 2022, the format was acquired by Netflix and retooled, achieving 50 million hours of viewing within three weeks. Ekta Kapoor, founder of Balaji Telefilms, credited Netflix for supporting varied content tailored to India's broad spectrum of viewers.

Prime Video has also expanded its unscripted slate. Nikhil Madhok, head of Indian Originals at Prime Video and Amazon MGM Studios India, pointed to *The Traitors* as a catalyst for unscripted reinvention in 2025, focusing on gameplay, strategy, and manipulation. The Indian version features Karan Johar as presenter, and a Telugu adaptation has been hosted by Teja Sajja, with the BBC acquiring the show for its iPlayer platform in the United Kingdom.

According to Vivek Couto, founder and CEO of Media Partners Asia, streaming platforms are managing risk by acquiring proven audiences rather than originating expensive formats from zero. Couto noted that India is no longer only adapting formats, but becoming a market where they are built and exported.
