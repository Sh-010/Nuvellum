Autonomous vehicle companies operating in California will face new rules, local support requirements, and potential financial penalties under legislation signed into law by Governor Gavin Newsom. Senate Bill 1246 aims to improve response times and accountability when self-driving vehicles experience system failures or interfere with emergency personnel.

## New accountability and penalties

Under the legislation, which takes effect in July 2028, autonomous vehicle operators could face fines if their vehicles block police officers or firefighters for more than 30 minutes during an emergency. The law also requires companies to notify local jurisdictions about vehicle locations and statuses during system-wide failures, and to supply local incident technicians to assist with accidents and obstructions.

The measure follows a series of incidents in California where disabled robotaxis disrupted traffic, entered crime scenes, or impeded emergency responders. Waymo, the largest commercial robotaxi operator in the United States, accounted for the bulk of those occurrences. State Senator Dave Cortese, who introduced the measure, stated that while California embraces innovation, public safety must not be compromised.

## Remote operations standards

The legislation also establishes specific requirements for remote operations. Companies employing remote drivers—defined specifically as humans who directly operate or drive a vehicle from afar—must base those workers in the United States and require them to hold a U.S. driver's license.

Industry practices regarding remote intervention vary. Tesla has reported having remote operators capable of taking direct control of its robotaxis. Meanwhile, companies such as Waymo and Zoox utilize remote assistance methods where the autonomous system remains in control while human employees provide guidance or software commands. Waymo maintains a global network of remote assistance staff, including personnel in the Philippines and command centers in Arizona and Michigan, while Zoox states its remote operations teams are based domestically.

Representatives for Waymo and Zoox stated they intend to comply with the new requirements. The California Department of Motor Vehicles is tasked with establishing guidelines on specific aspects of the legislation, including mandatory response times.
