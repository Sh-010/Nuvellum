## The setup of season 5 episode 4

Twenty-six years ago, the *Buffy the Vampire Slayer* episode titled "Out of My Mind" premiered on The WB. Written by Rebecca Rand Kirshner and directed by David Grossman, the fourth episode of the show's fifth season laid groundwork for multiple plot threads. 

In the episode, Spike (played by James Marsters) is introduced as harmless after having a brain chip installed that prevents him from hurting humans. While Buffy (Sarah Michelle Gellar) intends to stake him, she holds back during a confrontation, and Spike kisses her in a dream sequence. The episode concludes with Spike waking up horrified, shouting, "Oh God, no. Please, no," as the credits roll.

## Riley's struggles and narrative threads

Alongside the central dynamic between Spike and Buffy, the episode focuses heavily on Buffy's college boyfriend, Riley (Marc Blucas). Riley attempts to prove his strength by recklessly fighting vampires. Buffy later discovers that drugs given to Riley by The Initiative put him at risk of a heart attack.

While Buffy searches for Riley, who has gone into hiding, Spike takes bribe money from Buffy but uses it to kidnap the doctor meant to help Riley. Spike attempts to force the doctor to remove his brain inhibitor, though the plan fails. Meanwhile, tensions rise between Buffy and Riley, culminating in exchanges that precede their eventual breakup and Riley's departure from the series.

## The evolution of Spike's character

"Out of My Mind" establishes a more vulnerable and lovelorn version of Spike, who spends his free time watching *Dawson's Creek* while unable to harm humans. The episode's final twist revealed that Spike's obsession with Buffy involved more complicated feelings than straightforward murderous rage.

Reflecting on the writing process, series writer David Fury noted in a past interview that the moment Buffy discovers Spike's feelings later in the season evolved unexpectedly from the writers' initial plans to let other characters find out first. In hindsight, the dream sequence's kiss serves as an early representation of the pair's dynamic throughout the series: Spike's persistent affection contrasted with Buffy keeping him emotionally at arm's length.
