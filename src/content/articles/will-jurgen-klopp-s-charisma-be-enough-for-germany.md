Following a defeat to Greece, Germany head coach Jürgen Klopp faces the real challenge of his new role. After a shock loss left the team with just one point after their first two games, questions have emerged about whether his charisma alone can push the squad forward.

## Klopp shields players and seeks time

Klopp took responsibility for the loss, aiming to shield his new group of players from criticism. "We have been together for a week now, all mixed together, and that needs time," Klopp said after the match, adding that established players have his support while urging critics to focus their attention on him instead.

Emphasizing development over short-term panic, Klopp noted that more training sessions are necessary to cement the automatic responses required to break down well-organized defenses. Bambase Conte and Hennes Behrens made their debuts against Greece following a draw against the Netherlands, marking early steps toward identifying players for Euro 2028.

## The limits of international management

Unlike club management, where Klopp spent years shaping teams like Liverpool through extensive training sessions and targeted player acquisitions, international coaches face severe constraints. Klopp will have approximately 200 training sessions before Euro 2028, compared to the roughly 500 sessions he utilized during his first two years at Liverpool.

Furthermore, international managers cannot sign new players from the transfer market, though Klopp has utilized first-time call-ups by selecting 11 uncapped players in his initial squad. While Klopp's communication style and focus on team culture align with what the national team requires, it remains to be seen whether this approach can compensate for squad capabilities over the next two to three years.
