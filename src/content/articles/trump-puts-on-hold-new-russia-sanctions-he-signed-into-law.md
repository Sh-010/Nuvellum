## Executive order on sanctions

US President Donald Trump has put on hold a sweeping package of sanctions on Russia that he signed into law last month. Trump told Congress that the pause is in the national interest, signing an executive order on Friday just two days before a deadline in the legislation that would have brought the sanctions into effect.

The bipartisan law was named after the late Republican Senator Lindsey Graham, who passed away unexpectedly in July after returning from Ukraine. Drafted by Graham and Democratic Senator Richard Blumenthal, the legislation had previously passed through both the House of Representatives and the Senate by large margins.

The measure aimed to reduce funding for Moscow's war effort by targeting Russian officials, banks, and a shadow fleet of oil tankers. It also instructed Trump to levy tariffs of up to 100 percent on the five largest importers of Russian oil or gas, while providing an exception for nations importing under 15 percent of Russia's natural gas exports that are actively reducing those purchases.

## Diesel deal and political reaction

The decision to suspend the sanctions follows an announcement a week prior in which Trump stated the US had secured a diesel purchase agreement with Russia in an effort to reduce global fuel prices.

Lawmakers from both major parties criticized the arrangement, arguing it would assist Russia's war effort without altering fuel prices. Democratic Representative Don Beyer stated that he and others had anticipated Trump's move and urged Congress to amend the law to prevent Trump from accommodating Russian President Vladimir Putin and to ensure it supports Ukraine.

Additionally, Trump has recently increased criticism of Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy for not reaching an agreement with Russia. Ahead of the November 3 midterm elections, as Republicans seek to retain control of Congress, an AP-NORC poll published on October 1 indicated that Americans disapprove of Trump's handling of the Iran war and attribute higher prices to his policies.
