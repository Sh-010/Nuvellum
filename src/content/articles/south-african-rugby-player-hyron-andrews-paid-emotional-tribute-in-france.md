## Tributes held in France and South Africa

Players bowed their heads and held a moment of silence for South African rugby player Hyron Andrews before a Champions Cup game between French side La Rochelle and the Johannesburg-based Lions. Andrews died at the age of 31 in France on the eve of the fixture.

The match went ahead at the Marcel-Deflandre Stadium in La Rochelle after discussions between the two teams. La Rochelle players wore black armbands, and the French club's backs coach, Remi Tales, noted that some La Rochelle players visited the team hotel to offer support to the Lions players. The crowd observed a moment of silence, breaking into spontaneous applause, and La Rochelle handed a club shirt to the Lions staff. The match ended in a 31-14 victory for La Rochelle.

SA Rugby and the Lions confirmed the death of the lock forward. The number five shirt previously worn by Andrews was placed on the field, and his face was shown on the stadium's big screen with his full name, Hyron Diego Andrews. A moment of silence was also observed at a match in Pretoria between the Bulls and Stade Francais.

## Investigation into the death

Local authorities and prosecutors stated that Andrews was found dead on October 16 at a hotel in Sainte-Marie-de-Re in southwestern France. La Rochelle prosecutor Arnaud Laraize told The Associated Press that Andrews was discovered in a shower cubicle locked from the inside, roughly an hour after completing a hammam session. A source close to the investigation reported that the player was found in cardiac arrest.

According to his entourage, Andrews had shown no signs of health problems in the preceding days. An investigation into the cause of death was opened, and an autopsy was scheduled.

## Career and remembrances

SA Rugby reported that Andrews died following a fall. Throughout his career, Andrews also represented the KZN Sharks in South Africa and Sale Sharks in England, making 45 appearances for the latter.

Rudolf Straeuli, CEO of SA Rugby, described Andrews as a gifted player and a cherished member of the Lions family who had recently joined the side. Sale Sharks also paid tribute, with Director of Rugby Alex Sanderson remembering him as a brilliant teammate and a charismatic individual.
