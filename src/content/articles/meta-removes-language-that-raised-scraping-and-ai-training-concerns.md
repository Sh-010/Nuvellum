## Commercial terms update

Meta recently updated its commercial terms of use to state that the company could utilize content from business account domains, web pages, or URLs to provide, develop, improve, research, train, and maintain the integrity of its products and services.

Legal experts interviewed by Nieman Lab described the provision as remarkable, suggesting it could grant Meta permission to scrape publisher websites connected to a Meta business account and use that material to train artificial intelligence models. While Meta successfully defended against a high-profile copyright infringement lawsuit regarding AI training on books last year, separate legal challenges involving publishers and AI companies continue.

## Provision removed

Following questions over the policy, a Meta spokesperson maintained that the updated terms did not signify any shift in how the company crawls the web. 

According to reports from The Verge, the contentious provision has since been eliminated. In a statement, the company said it heard feedback and clarified that the consolidated crawling terms applied strictly to ads and business products, noting that the update does not alter how Meta employs web crawlers.
