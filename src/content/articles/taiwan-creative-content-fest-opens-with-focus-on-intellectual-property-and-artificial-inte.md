The seventh edition of the Taiwan Creative Content Fest opened with a focus on strengthening local intellectual property, expanding international markets through co-productions, and reassuring industry figures of government backing.

## Addressing Industry Changes and Artificial Intelligence

Sue Wang, chair of the organizer Taiwan Creative Content Agency, opened the event by addressing global industry disruptions over the past year. She noted that creation now requires maximizing intellectual property universes and adapting to continuous changes. 

Artificial intelligence also formed a major focal point during the opening proceedings. Alongside signing a memorandum of understanding with Weta FX to position Taiwan as a regional hub, organizers emphasized the necessity of human involvement. Wang stated that creators must evaluate "what are the elements that cannot be replaced by AI."

## Government Funding and International Presence

Taiwan Minister of Culture Li Yuan addressed attendees regarding the creative sector's financial backing, highlighting a second tranche of investment from the National Development Fund. The funds are currently supporting films, series, comics, publications, animation, streaming platforms, and corporate entities.

The market section of the event hosts 107 exhibitors representing seven countries and regions, including a first-time national pavilion from Thailand. The festival runs at the Taipei Nangang Exhibition Center, featuring pitching sessions, a market, forums, startup fundraising pitches, and spotlight screenings.
