## Festival stampede in Kolkata

A stampede during a Hindu religious festival in eastern India left at least 17 people dead and 25 injured, according to officials. West Bengal Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari stated that a seven-year-old girl was among the fatalities following the incident on Saturday in north Kolkata.

"Six bodies have so far been identified," Adhikari told reporters.

Police reported that the injured individuals were transported to a hospital from a Durga Puja pandal, which is a temporary pavilion housing idols of the Hindu goddess Durga. The festival typically draws thousands of devotees.

## Incident causes and official response

According to a police statement, the stampede occurred in the evening as a result of "heavy crowds of pandal hoppers amid rainfall." A senior police official at the scene noted that an announcement created a "commotion" after rain began, leading the waiting crowd outside the pavilion to push, pull, and rush forward. People fell to the ground, triggering the stampede.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi characterized the event as "saddening" in a post on X, offering condolences to the families of the victims and wishing the injured a recovery. Modi's office announced financial compensation of 200,000 rupees ($2,106) for the next of kin of those killed and 50,000 rupees ($527) for each injured person.

Interior Minister Amit Shah stated that the local administration was working to ensure medical facilities were supplied to the injured.

## Frequent crowd incidents

Deadly crowd disasters occur regularly at mass gatherings and religious festivals in India, frequently driven by safety lapses and management issues. Previous incidents include a stampede at the Kumbh Mela religious fair that killed 30 people, and a gathering in Uttar Pradesh in 2024 that resulted in 121 deaths.
