A new documentary directed by Judd Apatow, titled *Norm: The Tale of Norm Macdonald*, premieres Friday on Netflix. The film explores the life of the late Canadian comedian using his own words—largely drawn from his biographical book *Based on a True Story: Not a Memoir*—alongside home videos, photographs, and archival interviews, avoiding traditional talking-head formats.

## The Weekend Update approach

Macdonald is widely regarded alongside Chevy Chase as one of the top Weekend Update hosts in *Saturday Night Live* history. In the documentary, Macdonald credits longtime collaborator and producer Lori Jo Hoekstra with helping shape the segment's distinct identity by bringing in a team of outside writers, including Ross Abrash and Frank Sebastiano, alongside writer Jim Downey.

“Lori Jo then suggested we hire outside writers so Weekend Update would have a completely different flavor from the rest of SNL,” Macdonald recounts in the film. “We all agreed that the jokes should be stripped of any cleverness, play on words or innuendo… A joke should catch people by surprise. It should never pander.”

## Departure from Saturday Night Live

The circumstances surrounding Macdonald's exit from *Saturday Night Live* remain a central part of his career lore. During the 1990s, Macdonald and the Weekend Update team frequently targeted O.J. Simpson during his murder trial. Following Simpson's acquittal, Macdonald stated on the show: “Well, it is finally official: Murder is legal in the state of California.”

According to accounts featured in the documentary, then-NBC West Coast president Don Ohlmeyer, an acquaintance of Simpson, grew displeased with the routine and fired both Macdonald and Downey over the jokes. Macdonald stated that Ohlmeyer personally called to tell him he was being terminated because he was not funny. Ohlmeyer subsequently attempted to block promotional spots for Macdonald's 1998 film *Dirty Work* and tried to prevent Conan O'Brien from booking Macdonald as a late-night guest, though both efforts were unsuccessful.

## Concealing a cancer diagnosis

The documentary addresses Macdonald's medical history through interview clips from longtime friend Harland Williams, who notes that the comic experienced a stomach cancer battle in the 1980s. Macdonald was subsequently diagnosed with multiple myeloma in 2013, choosing to disclose the diagnosis only to his family, agent, and ex-wife while keeping it hidden from the public and close friends.

Macdonald explains in the film that he was inspired to keep his condition private by actor Richard Farnsworth, who kept a terminal cancer diagnosis secret before his death in 2000. Macdonald died from leukemia complications in 2021 at the age of 61.

## Addressing the #MeToo movement

The film briefly touches on public backlash Macdonald faced late in his career, specifically regarding a 2018 interview with *The Hollywood Reporter* where he defended fellow comedians Louis C.K. and Roseanne Barr and expressed gratitude that the #MeToo movement had slowed down. Macdonald subsequently apologized on Twitter, and his scheduled appearance on *The Tonight Show* was canceled following the remarks.
