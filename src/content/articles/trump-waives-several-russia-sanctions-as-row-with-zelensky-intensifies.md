US President Donald Trump has blocked numerous sanctions on Russia just two days before they were scheduled to take effect, deepening an ongoing diplomatic row with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky. 

The measures had been passed by the US Congress and signed into law by Trump in September. However, the US president issued an executive order stating that suspending several provisions served the US "national interest," a decision that immediately drew criticism from US lawmakers.

## Congressional backlash and Russian response

Democratic US Representative Don Beyer of Virginia criticized the executive order on social media, accusing the administration of capitulating to Moscow. Beyer stated that the action "betrays our Ukrainian partners and defies clear congressional intent of legislation designed to punish Russia for its illegal war of aggression."

Conversely, Russian envoy Kirill Dmitriev praised the waiver on X as "an important and truly historic moment," claiming that full implementation would have caused serious damage to the US and global markets.

The suspended provisions included measures targeting senior Russian figures such as Vladimir Putin, Russian financial institutions, Moscow's "shadow fleet" of embargo-evading vessels, and broad powers to levy tariffs of up to 100% on imports from major purchasers of Russian oil and gas, primarily China and India.

## Fraying relations over energy and military support

Tensions between Washington and Kyiv have escalated following a controversial US deal to import Russian diesel aimed at lowering domestic energy prices ahead of US midterm elections. Zelensky suggested the move amounted to US investment in Russia's war effort.

Exchange of sharp words between the leaders followed, with Trump describing Zelensky as "more difficult to deal with" than Putin, while the Ukrainian president told an EU summit it was nice to be among "real leaders." Zelensky also voiced concern over unexpected restrictions on the Starlink satellite network used by Ukrainian forces, though Trump denied deliberately slowing connections or limiting intelligence-sharing.

## Ongoing attacks and future talks

Zelensky expressed disappointment over the waivers, arguing that the legislation "could have helped enormously" by placing effective pressure on Russia to end the conflict. His remarks followed overnight Russian attacks on 15 Ukrainian energy facilities that caused regional power cuts ahead of winter.

Ukrainian authorities reported that at least nine people have been killed and roughly 60 injured in strikes across the country over the past 24 hours, including an ambulance driver killed in Kherson. Meanwhile, Russia's defence ministry stated its operations targeted industries linked to Ukraine's military infrastructure, including steel works, an electrical substation, and a cargo vessel in the Black Sea.

Zelensky indicated that Ukrainian and US negotiating teams could meet next week, while expressing his belief that the Trump administration may lift further sanctions against Russia.
