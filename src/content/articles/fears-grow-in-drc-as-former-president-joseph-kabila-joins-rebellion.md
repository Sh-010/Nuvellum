## A new coalition in eastern Congo

Hopes for peace in the Democratic Republic of the Congo have suffered a setback after former President Joseph Kabila joined a new political-military coalition challenging President Felix Tshisekedi. Announced in Goma, the Front for the Refoundation of the Nation (FRN) replaces the existing alliance between the Alliance Fleuve Congo (AFC) and the March 23 Movement (M23).

The coalition accuses Tshisekedi of tyranny and calls for resistance by all means, including force. Goma, the main city in North Kivu province, has been under M23-AFC control since January of the previous year. Kabila’s move brings a former head of state directly into the leadership of the rebellion, raising questions about the conflict, which has displaced millions and drawn in neighbouring countries.

The conflict's roots involve regional rivalries, unresolved security concerns, and competition for territory and mineral resources. The aftermath of Rwanda’s 1994 genocide helped fuel regional wars, while tensions between Kinshasa and Kigali continue to shape the fighting. M23 states it took up arms because the Congolese government failed to honour earlier commitments. Meanwhile, the Congolese government and international actors have accused Rwanda of supporting the group, an allegation Kigali denies while citing security threats from armed groups operating in eastern Congo.

## Reactions and peace initiatives

Kabila’s involvement has drawn criticism from within the Congolese political establishment. Vital Kamerhe, a former speaker of the National Assembly, urged the movement to abandon armed struggle and prioritize dialogue. Last year, a Congolese military court sentenced Kabila to death in his absence on charges including treason and war crimes, a conviction his relatives described as unjust and politically motivated. Government spokesman Patrick Muyaya previously stated on X that Kabila had nothing to offer but chaos and described him as a man of the past.

International actors have pursued separate diplomatic initiatives to end the conflict. The United States and Qatar have engaged in separate efforts involving ceasefire discussions, refugee returns, detainee releases, and commitments concerning the neutralisation of the Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Rwanda and the withdrawal of Rwandan troops from Congolese territory.

The newly formed FRN stated that it will assume the AFC's rights, obligations, and commitments, specifically those entered into during the Doha negotiations. However, local residents and human rights activists, such as Josue Akuzwe Walayi, expressed fears that the realignment could threaten the future of the Doha and Washington peace initiatives and prolong the suffering of the population.
