At its annual DevDay conference, OpenAI announced a new AI agent platform named Dots. CEO Sam Altman introduced the system during his keynote, describing Dots as a real-deal personal assistant powered by GPT-6 Astra and designed with a customizable, colorful, blob-like appearance featuring eyes.

## Targeting complex work and professional use cases

OpenAI positioned Dots as a tool for handling ambitious professional tasks, such as website design, team offsite trip planning, QA for unreleased products, portfolio rebalancing, and email campaign creation. Executives stated that the platform is built to stay on track over long periods and handle complex knowledge-work assignments. Altman and other leaders contrasted Dots with consumer-facing competitors by emphasizing its long-horizon capabilities and upcoming specialist versions trained for accounting and legal analysis.

## Pricing and competitive hurdles against free alternatives

A primary challenge for Dots is its pricing structure. While Meta offers its Muse AI agent platform for free with a dedicated virtual machine for users, OpenAI restricts Dots to subscribers on its $20-per-month Pro plan and higher. Company representatives noted that Dots requires significant compute resources, though Altman indicated that a mass-market version for billions of users could be developed in the future.

## Focus on security and privacy controls

OpenAI executives also highlighted security and privacy features for the new platform, introducing the "OpenAI Private Intelligence" framework. This system provides businesses with controls including zero data retention policy options. Company leadership contrasted this approach with competitor products, pointing to deliberate controls regarding how agents interact with user identity, plug-ins, and financial transactions to avoid errors.
