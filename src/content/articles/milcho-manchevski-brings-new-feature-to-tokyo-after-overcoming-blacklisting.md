Macedonian filmmaker Milcho Manchevski is screening his ninth feature, *Sister Brother Manhole Cover*, in competition at the Tokyo Film Festival. The film follows two siblings on the outskirts of post-Communist North Macedonia who make a living stealing bronze busts and manhole covers. After a routine theft goes wrong, they navigate the local underworld following a deal with a corrupt doctor.

## Overcoming a Blacklist

The release marks Manchevski's second film of the year, following his documentary *Good People*, which debuted at the Sarajevo Festival. The director noted that having two releases simultaneously is unusual, but welcomed the productivity after years of being unable to produce work in his home country. 

Manchevski stated that he was blacklisted and faced halted projects, criminal charges, and a smear campaign after reporting corruption at the Macedonian Film Agency. According to the filmmaker, his accusations were later confirmed by the nation's Anti-Corruption Commission and State Audit Office, and he subsequently won two court cases against the agency.

## Production and Themes

*Sister Brother Manhole Cover* was filmed entirely on location around Skopje, including a constructed slum near an ancient aqueduct and a sanitized section of a real rubbish dump. International co-producers on the project include entities from Bulgaria, Serbia, Albania, the United Kingdom, and Italy, with support from various national film centers.

Reflecting on the contemporary film industry, Manchevski expressed a preference for working in Europe due to greater artistic independence, while criticizing modern pressures from festivals and funds to produce ideological, message-driven cinema.
