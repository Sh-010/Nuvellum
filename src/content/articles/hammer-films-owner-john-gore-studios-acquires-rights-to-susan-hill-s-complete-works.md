## Acquisition of Susan Hill's literary catalogue

John Gore Studios, the owner of British horror icon Hammer Films, has acquired the complete rights to the works of author Dame Susan Hill. The acquisition spans Hill's publishing career of more than six decades, encompassing her literary fiction, crime writing, children’s books, memoirs, radio drama, and her 1983 Gothic horror novel, *The Woman in Black*.

*The Woman in Black* previously inspired a stage adaptation that ran in London’s West End for 33 years, as well as a 2012 Hammer feature film starring Daniel Radcliffe. That film grossed $127.7 million, establishing itself as the highest-grossing British horror film in two decades and generating the largest U.S. opening in Hammer’s history. Hill's wider literary career includes her 1972 novel *The Bird of Night*, which was shortlisted for the Booker Prize, alongside honors such as the Somerset Maugham Award, the Whitbread Novel Award, and an election as a Fellow of the Royal Society of Literature.

## Plans for new adaptations and portfolio management

Following the purchase, John Gore Studios plans to exploit Hill's portfolio across film and television, alongside theater opportunities where existing rights arrangements permit. Founder and executive chairman John Gore stated that the studio's ambition is to approach the material with care and respect for character, suspense, and atmosphere.

Susan Hill will remain actively involved in new creative projects as an executive producer and will participate in the ongoing management and development strategy. The transaction was brokered by Caroline Michel, literary agent and CEO of Peters Fraser and Dunlop (PFD), who will continue to represent John Gore Studios as owner of the works.

The acquisition agreement was led by John Gore Studios Chief Operating Officer Anouska Spiers, who will oversee the portfolio management and overall exploitation strategy. Development will be managed by Chief Creative Officer Francis Hopkinson alongside the studio's creative team.
