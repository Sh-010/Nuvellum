The 1982 horror film *Poltergeist* is available to stream on HBO Max, bringing renewed attention to one of cinema's most famous behind-the-scenes controversies regarding its actual director.

## A suburban haunting

The film follows an ordinary suburban family experiencing bizarre phenomena in their home, from moving furniture to their five-year-old daughter, Carol Anne, communicating with unknown entities through the television. After a tree snatches the young boy outside and a portal in the closet pulls Carol Anne into another dimension, the family enlists paranormal investigators to bring her back.

## The directorial debate

While Tobe Hooper is officially credited as the director, an ongoing debate persists over whether producer and co-writer Steven Spielberg was the true creative force behind the camera.

Origins of the project trace back to Spielberg's unmade sci-fi horror film *Night Skies*, which he brought Hooper in to potentially direct. Accounts diverge on whether Hooper shifted the concept into *Poltergeist* or came up with the idea entirely on his own. During production, Spielberg remained heavily involved on set, creating the impression of two directors due to his hands-on, vocal style contrasting with Hooper's quieter approach.

Cast and crew members have offered split accounts over the years. Camera assistant John Leonetti stated that Spielberg directed the film, whereas stars JoBeth Williams and Craig T. Nelson affirmed Hooper was the director while acknowledging Spielberg's active presence as a producer. Medium actor Zelda Rubinstein characterized the production as a joint venture where Spielberg held slightly more control.

Studio marketing that heavily promoted Spielberg's name and contemporary interviews—such as Spielberg describing his own active intervention when answers were not immediately forthcoming—further complicated the picture. The Director's Guild eventually investigated the matter, determining that MGM and United Artists had denigrated Hooper's title and forcing the studio to pay him $15,000.

## Style and legacy

Critics and observers point to elements of both filmmakers in the final product: the accessible, heartwarming suburban family focus associated with Spielberg alongside genuinely terrifying sequences, such as a face-peeling effect and a muddy pit of bodies, that align with Hooper's style. Decades later, the film remains widely regarded as a standout feature of the 1980s.
