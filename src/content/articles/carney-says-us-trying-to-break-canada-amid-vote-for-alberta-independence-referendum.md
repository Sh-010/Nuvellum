## Alberta vote on separation process

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney warned residents of the oil-rich province of Alberta that the United States is attempting to fracture the country so it can take ownership of it. Carney delivered the remarks at a pro-Canada rally in Calgary ahead of a provincial vote.

The upcoming vote is not a direct referendum on independence. Instead, the electorate in Alberta—which holds the world's fourth-largest proven oil reserves—will determine whether to stay within Canada or instruct the government to initiate the legal process toward a separation referendum.

Albertans have long expressed grievances regarding federal power, immigration policies, and management of the oil industry under liberal governments in Ottawa. Carney, who grew up in Alberta, acknowledged these frustrations during his campaign stop, stating that Ottawa had previously made residents feel that their resources were not their own. However, he urged voters against treating the ballot as a protest.

"Don't be fooled by the idea you can 'send a message' with your vote," Carney said. "This is not poker. A vote against Canada is a dangerous bluff that will lead to years of deep division and great uncertainty."

## Trade tensions and US relations

Carney’s warning comes amid a trade war between Ottawa and Washington, alongside threats from US President Donald Trump regarding Canada's status. Carney has pursued policies aimed at reducing Canada's economic dependence on the United States, drawing pushback from Washington.

While Carney warned of external pressures without directly naming the US or its president in his opening remarks, separatist figures in Alberta disputed external involvement in the current vote. Mitch Sylvestre, leader of the separatist group Alberta Prosperity Project, stated that US interests do not play a role in the province until independence is achieved. He noted there is no animosity toward America and expressed a desire to maintain free-flowing trade.

At the same time, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has described Alberta as a natural partner for the United States, and leaders of the Alberta Prosperity Project have held meetings at the US State Department.
