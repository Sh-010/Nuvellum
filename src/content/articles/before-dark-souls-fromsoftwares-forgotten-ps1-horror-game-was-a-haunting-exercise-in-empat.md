Long before FromSoftware became globally celebrated for unforgiving action RPGs like *Dark Souls* and *Elden Ring*, the studio's early catalog featured distinct experiments in atmosphere and terror. Alongside titles like *Kuon*, *Shadow Tower*, and *Eternal Ring*, the 1998 PlayStation release *Echo Night* offered a radical departure from conventional survival horror of its era.

## A ghost story without combat

Written and produced by Toshifumi Nabeshima, *Echo Night* rejected the blood, slaughtered gods, and resource scarcity popularized by games like *Resident Evil*. Instead, players step into the shoes of Richard Osmond, whose search for his missing father leads to a mysterious painting that transports him onto the *Orpheus*, a long-lost ocean liner.

Rather than fighting hostile spirits with weapons, players navigate pitch-black rooms to find light switches, as illumination serves as the primary defense against malice. Gameplay progression centers on modular tragedy and observation. Players help restless passengers resolve lingering regrets—such as tracking down a missing ring for a grieving widow or solving riddles for trapped sailors—receiving an Astral Piece each time a soul finds eternal rest.

## A trilogy of overlooked experiments

The formula of *Echo Night* formed a trilogy that remained largely obscure outside of niche horror communities. The Japan-exclusive *Echo Night 2: Lord of Nightmares* launched in 1999, shifting the ghostly investigation to a gothic mansion and sharpening Nabeshima's focus on interconnected character vignettes. Later, in 2004, *Echo Night: Beyond* brought the series to the sixth console generation, placing players inside an abandoned lunar research base featuring suit-oxygen mechanics.

## Tracing the studio's DNA

Critics at the time occasionally polarized over mechanics like *Beyond*'s slow pace, but the franchise established narrative sensibilities that carry through to FromSoftware's modern blockbusters. The mournful environmental storytelling and fragmented lore seen in *Dark Souls* and *Elden Ring* share direct roots with *Echo Night*, requiring players to piece together forgotten tragedies independently rather than relying on exposition-heavy cutscenes.
