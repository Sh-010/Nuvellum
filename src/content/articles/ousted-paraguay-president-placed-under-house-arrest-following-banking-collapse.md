## Senate impeachment and house arrest

Paraguayan authorities placed ousted president Santiago Peña under house arrest on Friday, shortly after the Senate voted unanimously to remove him from office. All 44 senators present voted for his removal due to misconduct following the collapse of a government-linked bank that froze access to deposits for nearly half of the country's population. Although Peña offered his resignation ahead of the verdict, lawmakers proceeded with the impeachment trial, stripping him of his political immunity.

Peña faces accusations including breach of trust, influence peddling, and using public office for personal gain. Prosecutors ordered him to remain at home in Asunción pending a court appearance. Lawmakers allege that he directed hundreds of millions in public funds into Ueno Bank while regulatory bodies overlooked risky lending practices. Peña has denied any wrongdoing.

## Banking collapse and public fallout

Regulators seized Ueno Bank after a solvency crisis locked millions of dollars in customer accounts. The digital lender had rapidly expanded, acquiring over a million accounts by targeting unbanked citizens with targeted promotions and partnerships, including sponsorship of the national soccer team. The sudden freeze trapped personal savings, state pension funds, and public infrastructure loans, triggering widespread protests and street demonstrations in Asunción.

Following the removal of Peña, Vice President Pedro Alliana was sworn in to complete the remainder of the presidential term through August 2028. Alliana acknowledged the urgent demands facing his administration as it works to address frozen deposits and public accountability. Meanwhile, prosecutors have launched legal proceedings against numerous bank executives and former officials linked to the institution's management.
