Finance officials from around the world are gathering in Bangkok for the annual meetings of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and the World Bank. The event marks the first time in three years that the meetings are being held outside of Washington, drawing 18,000 registered attendees.

## Conflict and economic risks

The agenda is dominated by the US-Israel war on Iran, which is in its eighth month, alongside rising interest rates and energy supply shocks. IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva noted that while overall global growth is forecast to hold steady at three percent in 2026, downgrades are expected for specific regions, including Ukraine and Gulf countries affected by strikes and reduced energy exports. 

United States Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent will not attend, sending two senior officials instead to handle domestic engagements. Federal Reserve Board Chairman Kevin Warsh is scheduled to attend and participate in a public event with Georgieva.

## Global debt and emergency energy releases

Public debt levels are highlighted as a major challenge, with the IMF reporting that debt has reached its highest level since World War II and is projected to exceed 100 percent of global gross domestic product before 2030. Developing nations face approximately $400bn in external debt payments in 2026, alongside interest payments exceeding 10 percent of revenue on average. Lower-income countries have expressed concern over new IMF loan recommendations that require fewer but deeper structural reforms.

Meanwhile, Group of Seven countries have agreed to release 100 million barrels of diesel and crude oil from emergency reserves. The decision follows pressure from United States President Donald Trump aimed at lowering petrol prices. Additionally, a US-announced deal with Russia aims to provide further diesel to global markets through a temporary waiver of sanctions, a move that drew criticism from Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy.
