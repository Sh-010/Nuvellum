## Missile strike hits Aden presidential palace

Yemen’s Houthis targeted the vicinity of the al-Maashiq presidential palace in the port city of Aden with multiple missiles. Yemeni Information Minister Moammar al-Eryani stated on social media that five missiles were fired at the palace, which serves as the headquarters and seat of power for the internationally recognised, Saudi-backed government.

The government reported that the prime minister and other officials are safe, but two bodyguards were killed in the attack. Plumes of smoke rose near the compound following explosions reported by correspondents.

Al Jazeera correspondent Yousef Mawry noted that the attacks mark the first of their kind against the headquarters of the internationally recognised government by the Houthis, raising concerns over a potential escalation and intensified civil conflict.

## Clashes in Taiz and coalition accusations

The attack in Aden coincided with intensified fighting between the Houthis and Saudi-backed forces in Taiz, which is effectively under siege by the Houthis. Control over Taiz and the broader governorate is strategic for securing the Bab al-Mandeb strait. Pro-government forces, including the Homeland Shield Force and the Southern Giants Brigades, stated they are repelling a Houthi attack and securing Dhubab airport in the Taiz governorate.

Aid agencies warned that the violence is straining the local healthcare system, with the Ministry of Public Health warning of a humanitarian catastrophe.

Meanwhile, the Saudi-led military coalition held a news conference in Riyadh, where spokesperson Major-General Turki al-Maliki stated that Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport has faced at least 20 Houthi attacks over an 11-day period, alongside strikes on other airports, oil facilities, and ports. The coalition reported that Houthi attacks have killed 17 people and injured more than 300, and accused the group of obtaining missile parts and ballistic missiles from regional states.
