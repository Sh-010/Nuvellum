Director Danny Boyle received the Deadline Legend Award during Deadline’s Contenders Film Los Angeles awards-season event, where he discussed his extensive filmography, his latest directorial project, and future franchise plans.

## Unintentional Patterns and ‘Ink’

Reflecting on his career, Boyle admitted he previously believed he never repeated himself across genres like *Trainspotting*, *127 Hours*, and *Slumdog Millionaire*. However, a critique from a French journalist who argued that all his films share the same fundamental architecture—focusing on a protagonist facing insurmountable odds—changed his perspective. He noted that filmmakers often try to create distinct projects, but underlying structural similarities tend to emerge.

Boyle’s latest feature, *Ink*, adapts James Graham’s Tony-winning play about Rupert Murdoch and editor Larry Lamb. The film details their efforts to revive a British newspaper and challenge established competitors like *The Mirror*. Boyle explained that the project explores how the pair utilized tabloid techniques that resonated with audiences emerging from a postwar mentality, before shifting toward the consequences of those tactics. He also confirmed that certain headlines redacted in British versions due to libel laws will appear in United States releases.

## Artistic License and Franchise Updates

Addressing creative choices in *Ink*, Boyle defended the inclusion of a composite character and the use of archival footage of former Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher without estate approval, emphasizing the importance of artistic freedom regarding public figures.

Turning to the horror genre, Boyle provided an update on the *28 Years Later* franchise. Following recent entries, Alex Garland has completed a script for a third installment. Boyle stated that Cillian Murphy is prepared to reprise his role, noting that the script forms a well-structured conclusion to the narrative arc.
