## A shifting jury composition

For years, the United States held the largest presence by far of any single country on the voting jury for The Game Awards. In 2024 and 2025, American outlets occupied 24 seats on an international jury of more than 130 members. However, the composition of the 2026 jury reveals a dramatically reduced presence of U.S. media, with just 17 American outlets remaining.

At the same time, the United Kingdom increased its representation from 14 to 17 votes, drawing level with the United States for the first time. The shift follows a consistent trend of internationalization since the awards began in 2014.

## Global rise of Latin America and Europe

Another major development is the emergence of Brazil as a primary voting power. Brazil increased its footprint by four outlets to reach 14 votes, positioning itself just behind the British and Americans. This growth aligns with a continuing surge in jury members from Latin American countries, which now comprise approximately 21% of the voting body.

Meanwhile, mainland Europe has consolidated the membership increases it secured previously, closely rivaling English-language media at 27% to 28% of the jury, respectively. Conversely, the Anglophone voting bloc of the U.S., U.K., Canada, and Australia is on the wane, and the influence of East and Southeast Asia has dropped slightly, with China holding three votes.

## Departure of mainstream media

The sharp decline in American outlets is driven by a mass departure of mainstream, lifestyle, and tech media publications from the jury, including Entertainment Weekly, Esquire, Inverse, Rolling Stone, The Atlantic, Wirecutter, and Wired.

While historical Game of the Year wins frequently went to American games—such as between 2014 and 2020, when U.S. games won four out of seven times—subsequent awards have gone to titles from Sweden, Belgium, France, and Japan. Observers note that upcoming high-profile releases like Grand Theft Auto 6, developed primarily by Rockstar North in Scotland with extensive American studio contributions and a U.S. setting, may test whether American-centric blockbusters can maintain prominence under the newly diversified international jury.
