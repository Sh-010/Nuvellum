## Diplomatic dispute over prisoner transfer

A diplomatic controversy has developed between Ukraine and South Korea regarding two North Korean prisoners of war. The issue arose after Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy publicly stated during a UN General Assembly speech on September 23 that Ukraine had transferred two North Korean prisoners of war to the Republic of Korea. The two men had been captured by Ukrainian forces in January 2025 during fighting in Russia's Kursk region and had expressed a desire to resettle in South Korea.

The announcement prompted a swift reaction from Seoul. South Korea's Foreign Ministry summoned Ukraine's ambassador, Andrii Vieshkin, demanding an explanation for what it termed Kyiv's "unilateral disclosure" of the transfer, along with an official apology. South Korean President Lee Jae Myung described the transfer as a sensitive topic carrying significant implications for diplomacy and national security on the Korean Peninsula.

## Conflicting accounts over confidentiality

Disagreements emerged between Kyiv and Seoul concerning whether an agreement to keep the transfer confidential had been established. Dmytro Lytvyn, a spokesperson for President Zelenskyy, stated that no such secrecy agreement existed and asserted it was incorrect to conceal the destinations of transferred prisoners.

Conversely, South Korean presidential spokesperson Kim Sung-wan disputed Lytvyn's denial, stating that Ukraine had requested confidentiality due to domestic concerns regarding potential criticism if the North Korean prisoners were not exchanged for Ukrainian prisoners held in Russia. According to Seoul, agreement to the secrecy was made for diplomatic reasons and to safeguard the prisoners and their families residing in North Korea.

## Reactions and domestic political context

Analysts note that the transfer has generated intense debate within South Korean media and domestic politics, where North Koreans are classified as South Korean citizens under the country's constitution. Opposition lawmakers have scrutinized President Lee's handling of the matter, while observers suggest Seoul's current administration is managing complex diplomatic relations involving North Korea and Russia.

Experts interviewed by Deutsche Welle offered differing perspectives on the fallout. Nataliya Butyrska, an Asia-Pacific expert at the New Europe Center in Kyiv, suggested the situation reflected a lack of continuous, trust-based dialogue between Ukraine and South Korea, adding that the prisoners could have been utilized to strengthen bilateral ties. Meanwhile, Mykola Polishchuk, a Ukraine-based Korea expert, indicated that the incident caught South Korean politicians off guard, leading to conflicting explanations regarding secrecy. Shim Sangmin of the Asan Institute for Policy Studies in Seoul noted that South Korea remains cautious about actions that might complicate efforts to reestablish communication with North Korea.
