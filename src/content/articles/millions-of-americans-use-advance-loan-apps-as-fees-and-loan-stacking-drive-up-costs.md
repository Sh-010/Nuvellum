## The rising popularity of paycheck advance apps

More than 10 million Americans used earned wage access or on-demand pay tools as of 2024, according to data from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. These tools allow workers to access portions of their earnings prior to payday to bridge financial gaps. According to a 2022 Government Accountability Office report, these services are primarily used by individuals earning less than $50,000, with food and rent serving as key reasons for utilization.

A report by the Center for Responsible Lending’s Julian Bond Institute analyzed bank transactions from 10,000 consumers and found that 96% of direct-to-consumer payday loan apps included fees or tips. These costs averaged nearly $208 per year, or about $17 per month. While these applications often market themselves as free and do not require credit checks or charge standard interest, they can require fees for fast transfers or memberships, alongside optional tips. According to the Julian Bond report, these charges can drive the effective annual percentage rate up to 232% for an average loan.

Transaction volumes for on-demand pay apps tripled from $3.2 billion to $9.5 billion between 2018 and 2020, according to Datos Insights. Tom Feltner, associate director of consumer policy at Americans for Financial Reform Education Fund, told Fortune that app usage has increased amid ongoing affordability concerns.

## The risks of loan stacking

Short-term financing tools, including buy-now-pay-later (BNPL) apps, have grown in popularity alongside inflation since the pandemic. LendingTree data indicates that nearly one-third of Americans have used BNPL services to finance necessities such as groceries, doubling the figure from two years prior.

Feltner noted that while individual transactions on these platforms are often small, the availability of multiple apps leads to "loan stacking." This practice involves consumers outsourcing short-term financing across multiple platforms simultaneously, which accumulates fees and raises the risk of missed payments and delinquency. CFPB data from 2025 indicated that over 60% of BNPL users maintained simultaneous loans, predominantly with lower or subprime credit scores.

Regulatory scrutiny surrounding these tools has shifted. In 2024, the CFPB proposed a rule requiring tips and fees to be disclosed as finance charges under an APR framework. However, the CFPB withdrew the proposal in December 2025, ruling that voluntary tips and fees do not constitute finance charges under regulatory guidelines.

## Broader economic impacts

Economists have raised concerns regarding the wider market effects of financing apps. Panos Kouvelis, a professor at the Olin Business School at Washington University in St. Louis, led a study modeling the economic impact of BNPL usage and retailer profits. The research indicated that retailers often increase sticker prices to offset merchant fees charged by these apps.

While BNPL tools were initially designed for discretionary purchases with higher profit margins, their expansion into basic goods such as groceries—which carry thinner margins—puts pressure on retailers. As a result, businesses face pressure to raise prices or reduce inventory options to manage the costs associated with short-term financing transactions.
