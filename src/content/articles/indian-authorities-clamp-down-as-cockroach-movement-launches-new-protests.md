## Protests and police response in New Delhi and Mumbai

Indian security forces clamped down as the Gen Z-led Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), student coalitions, and opposition forces gathered for planned demonstrations in New Delhi and Mumbai. Hundreds of police and paramilitary personnel surrounded a barricaded protest site near the Jantar Mantar observatory in central New Delhi on Friday, bundling demonstrators into buses.

Authorities closed at least 11 metro stations around the area and issued loudspeaker warnings to disperse. Delhi Police imposed Section 163, barring gatherings of more than five people, slogans, and speechmaking. Reports indicated that more than 500 people—including former Delhi Chief Minister Atishi Marlena, opposition members of the Aam Aadmi party, and student organisation members—were arrested in New Delhi. In Mumbai, a crowd grew in Shivaji Park amid a heavy police presence and a declared no-parking zone.

## Voter roll controversy and demands

The CJP called the demonstrations following a report by the Indian Express stating that Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar’s two deputies had raised concerns 14 times over 10 months regarding voter list revisions, registration procedures, and digital systems. Critics stated that a nationwide voter-verification exercise removed millions of eligible voters and favoured Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s ruling Bharatiya Janata Party.

The Election Commission denied the allegations, stating the adjustments removed duplicate, deceased, or ineligible voters. CJP convener Saurav Das stated that the movement plans to expand demonstrations nationwide until Kumar resigns. Opposition protests led by the Indian National Congress party are scheduled to begin on Tuesday.

## Background of the movement

India’s “cockroach movement” originated as online satire mocking the government before gaining millions of social media followers. The group previously organised mass demonstrations in July that resulted in the resignation of India’s education minister. Amnesty International reported that authorities used lethal weapons, including pellets and grenades, to suppress those earlier protests.
