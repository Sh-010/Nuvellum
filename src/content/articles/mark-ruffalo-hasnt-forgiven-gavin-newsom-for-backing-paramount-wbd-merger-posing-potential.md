## Continued friction over the Paramount-WBD merger

Actor Mark Ruffalo and outgoing California Governor Gavin Newsom remain at odds following the completion of David Ellison's mega-bucks Paramount and Warner Bros Discovery merger. Ruffalo, who spent months publicly opposing the deal, took to social media to criticize the centrist governor, writing that Newsom "sells out the working class people for the billionaires again and again."

The rift stems from both the media consolidation deal and state-level policy disagreements. Ruffalo has gained significant sway with Democratic party activists who fought against the Ellisons during the antitrust proceedings.

## Ballot measures and 2028 implications

The ongoing tensions could complicate a potential White House run for Newsom in 2028. The term-limited governor has not formally announced a presidential bid, stating he will decide after the midterms, though his travel and public messaging have pointed toward a national campaign.

Political operatives suggest that Newsom's positions on upcoming midterm ballot measures could hurt him with the party's progressive base. Newsom has come out against Proposition 40, a proposed wealth tax, while endorsing Proposition 42—a measure backed by Google co-founder Sergey Brin and other tycoons that aims to ban new state taxes on personal property and neutralize Proposition 40 if it passes.

Newsom has defended his opposition by warning that Proposition 40 could prompt wealthy residents to leave California. However, his stance has drawn sharp rebukes from fellow Democrats, including Representative Ro Khanna, who argued in a viral online video that pleasing donors and tech lords motivated Newsom's opposition. The Governor's office did not respond to requests for comment on Ruffalo's remarks.

## Professional overlap amid political division

Despite the political clash, professional ties remain active. Ruffalo is set to return in 2027 for the second season of the HBO crime drama *Task*, which falls under the Skydance umbrella following the completed merger.
