Following the Premier League's confirmation that Manchester City were found guilty of breaching financial rules over a nine-year period, former players and managers have expressed differing opinions regarding potential retrospective punishments and title adjustments.

## Keane and Rooney on retrospective titles

Former Manchester United captain Roy Keane stated that if he were a Manchester City player, he would throw his medals in the bin because the achievements were built on what he described as cheating. City were found guilty of flouting rules between 2009 and 2018 through "sham" contracts that funnelled more than £830m into football operations. Manchester City continue to deny any wrongdoing and plan to appeal the decision.

Wayne Rooney, a former Manchester United teammate of Keane, offered a different perspective on whether runners-up should inherit titles if City are stripped of them. Rooney stated that he would not feel comfortable accepting a retrospective Premier League medal for finishing second behind City in the 2011-2012 season, asserting that his team did not do enough that year to win the league.

## Views on relegation and sporting fairness

While Rooney opposed accepting redistributed medals, he voiced support for severe sporting sanctions, suggesting that Manchester City should face relegation to the lowest possible tier alongside spending restrictions for multiple seasons.

 Mauricio Pochettino, who managed several Premier League clubs during the period covered by the charges, described the situation as a "period of deception" that undermined sporting fairness. Pochettino questioned the enforcement of financial controls during his time managing Tottenham, noting that his club faced strict transfer restrictions while competing against rivals. La Liga president Javier Tebas also commented on the case, telling BBC Sport that the most severe sanctions permitted under Premier League regulations should be applied given the duration and repeated nature of the breaches.
