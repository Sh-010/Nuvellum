Diplomatic ties between Israel and European nations have grown increasingly strained amid the ongoing conflict in Gaza, raising questions about whether the upcoming October 27 Israeli election could alter relations. Once bound by extensive cultural, economic, and security cooperation—ranging from European participation in Eurovision to integrated weapons production—relations have faced severe friction following the destruction in Gaza and the rising death toll among Palestinians.

European governments have increasingly shifted from expressions of solidarity to direct criticism and measures such as settlement goods bans and diplomatic withdrawals. Analysts note that public pressure, driven by international protests and civil rights organisations, has compelled ruling elites in countries like the United Kingdom, Spain, and Ireland to align more closely with public discontent over the conflict.

## The outlook of the October 27 election

As Israel heads toward an election described as too close to call, experts remain divided on whether the vote will shift foreign policy. Yossi Mekelberg, a Middle East analyst at Chatham House, warned that a return to power by Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu alongside far-right factions would make current diplomatic damage deep and irreversible. Nicola Perugini, a senior lecturer at the University of Edinburgh, added that the re-election of a leader wanted by the International Criminal Court would likely sustain unliveable conditions in Gaza and further deteriorate ties with Europe.

While a coalition including more liberal parties might slow down settlement expansion and improve diplomatic dialogue, experts suggest it would not immediately break with the core policies drawing European criticism. Gadi Eisenkot, leader of the centrist Yashar party and a primary challenger to Netanyahu, has maintained opposition to Palestinian statehood. Julie M Norman, an associate fellow at Chatham House, noted that European governments would likely demand concrete steps toward Palestinian statehood and an end to the occupation—outcomes unlikely under current or immediate alternative leadership.

## Domestic priorities and public perception

Despite growing international isolation, diplomatic friction and foreign criticism remain largely absent from the central election debates inside Israel. Public discourse has instead focused heavily on security concerns, domestic living costs, military management of regional conflicts, and political accountability regarding the October 7 attacks.

Polling indicates strong domestic backing for military operations against regional adversaries. Analysts point out that public opinion inside Israel largely views foreign censure through the lens of hostility or ignorance rather than as a valid critique of state policy, leaving the fundamental direction of Israel's foreign relations largely insulated from external pressure during the electoral cycle.
