## Crash at the French tunnel entrance

Nineteen people were injured on Thursday when a minibus carrying truck drivers crashed into a vehicle shuttle train at the French entrance to the Channel Tunnel. The collision occurred in the town of Coquelles near Calais, according to local authorities.

The regional administration stated that the minibus was heading toward the tunnel entrance when it veered off course and struck the side of the shuttle train. Local official Agathe Cury noted that it was the first time a crash had taken place at the loading area for the trains that transport vehicles beneath the channel.

## Casualties and response

All 19 injured individuals had been aboard the minibus and are men of multiple nationalities, according to Cury, who stated that relevant consulates had been notified without specifying the specific countries involved. The minibus driver was among the injured.

Two people were hospitalised with serious but non-life-threatening injuries, while the remaining 17 sustained minor injuries. Approximately 200 emergency responders and other personnel deployed to the crash site.

## Service disruption and investigation

Vehicle shuttle services through the tunnel faced a 90-minute suspension before gradually resuming operations by late evening. Eurostar passenger trains and freight services remained unaffected by the incident.

Authorities stated that the cause of the crash remains unclear, and an official investigation is currently under way.
