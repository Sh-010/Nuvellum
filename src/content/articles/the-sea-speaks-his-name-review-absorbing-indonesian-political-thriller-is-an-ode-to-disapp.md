## A historical portrait of student resistance

Directed by Yosep Anggi Noen, *The Sea Speaks His Name* is adapted from a widely translated novel by Leila S. Chudori, who also serves as a producer. The film premiered in competition at the Busan Film Festival. Set predominantly in the 1990s, the narrative chronicles grassroots student resistance against military dictator Suharto during the final years of his New Order regime.

The story follows an idealistic young university student named Laut, played by Reza Rahadian. Introduced in a 1998 prologue, Laut is seized from his Jakarta apartment by government operatives during a power outage. The narrative then rewinds to 1991 in Yogyakarta, where Laut falls in with a campus discussion group led by a charismatic firebrand named Kinan, portrayed by Dian Sastrowardoyo. The group focuses on literature banned by the state.

## Narrative structure and performances

As the narrative progresses, the characters transition from discussion to a more actively radical student resistance movement. The regime's heavy-handed response forces Laut to adopt an alias and go into hiding. Following his 1998 capture, the film's focus shifts to his family's persistent efforts to piece together his story, with his sister Asmara—played by Yunita Siregar—taking up his cause.

The 134-minute feature reunites Noen with lead actor Reza Rahadian, previously featured in the 2024 Netflix crime drama *24 Hours With Gaspar*. The supporting cast features Christine Hakim as Laut's mother and Arswendy Bening Swara as his journalist father.

## Cinematic style and reception

Reviewers note that the film shares thematic similarities with Walter Salles' *I'm Still Here*, touching on forced disappearances under a military dictatorship. Noen previously explored anti-Suharto activism in his 2016 Locarno-premiered biopic *Solo, Solitude*.

Technically, the production utilizes ambiently somber cinematography by Batara Goempar, highlighting figures in shadows to reinforce the thriller elements. Dinda Amanda serves as editor, incorporating stirring archival footage at the conclusion of the PAL8 Pictures production.
