## Defamation lawsuit filed in federal court

A 15-year-old high school student from Maryland has filed a defamation lawsuit against CBS News in the U.S. District Court in the District of Columbia. The plaintiff, identified in the court filing as "JV," is seeking at least $20 million in damages.

According to the lawsuit, the teenager was riding his e-dirt bike past the National Mall on June 20, when he was briefly detained, handcuffed, and released without charges over his use of the e-bike. 

The legal action claims that a June 21 report on the *CBS Evening News* falsely stated the teenager was arrested for vandalizing the Lincoln Memorial reflecting pool. While his face was not shown on the television broadcast, the suit contends he was identifiable from the rear by his hair and a distinctive logo on his black hoodie.

## Online publication and retraction

The lawsuit states that CBS News also published the story on its website and social media platforms, including Facebook, TikTok, and Instagram, where the content reportedly amassed 1.8 million views. In the online version, an accompanying video showed the plaintiff's face as he turned around, leading family friends to recognize him and alert his family.

The plaintiff's attorneys stated that JV has experienced embarrassment, anxiety, sleep loss, emotional distress, and ongoing concern that the report could negatively impact his high school experience, college admissions, athletic opportunities, and future career.

On Aug. 21, CBS News anchor Jericka Duncan issued a retraction on air, stating, "We never should have published this story." A spokesperson for CBS News declined to comment on the matter.

## Vandalism allegations and dropped cases

Separate from the broadcast, the underlying allegations surrounding the reflecting pool vandalism have been dismissed. Aside from Olympian David Hearn, whose case was dropped after U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro acknowledged that damage to the pool resulted from faulty contractor work, no other individuals were charged.

An inspection report cited by *The Washington Post* concluded that the blue coating applied to the pool had failed in a minimum of 85 locations.
