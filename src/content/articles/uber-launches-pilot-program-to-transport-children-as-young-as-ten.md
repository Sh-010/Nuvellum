Ride-hailing company Uber has introduced a limited trial allowing children aged 10 and older to use its platform without an adult passenger. The initiative, known as Uber Kids, rolled out in recent weeks across Austin, Texas, Nashville, Tennessee, and Madison, Wisconsin.

## Safety requirements and features

The pilot program expands upon Uber's existing transport options for teenagers aged 13 to 17. Participating families must navigate mandatory onboarding processes and incorporate the child into a family profile. Only parents or guardians can request a trip, and an adult must be present at the pickup location alongside providing specific drop-off instructions.

Drivers selected for the initiative must maintain high ratings, receive a special invitation, and complete an educational module. Additional safety protocols mandate live video streaming capabilities for parents, PIN verification for every trip, and automated monitoring via GPS and sensors to detect unexpected stops or route deviations.

## Driver compensation

To address the added responsibilities involved with transporting younger passengers, participating drivers receive increased earnings. According to the company, eligible drivers earn an additional seven dollars per trip compared to standard fares, alongside a twenty-dollar bonus for completing their initial youth transport assignment. If successful, the trial could allow the corporation to tap into a new market of families while potentially competing with established student transport alternatives such as HopSkipDrive and Zum.
