## Opposition Unity and Strategy

Leaders from Israel's opposition change bloc are scheduled to meet on Tuesday in Herzliya for their first gathering since last week's election failed to provide a parliamentary majority for either major political faction. The closely contested vote left incumbent Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's right-wing alliance with 53 seats, while Gadi Eisenkot's bloc secured 48 seats in the 120-member Knesset.

All party leaders within Eisenkot's camp have endorsed him as their candidate for prime minister. A central point of discussion during Tuesday's meeting will involve how to coordinate future steps and whether to cooperate with Arab-majority lists, which achieved a record 19 seats and emerged as potential kingmakers.

## Netanyahu's Coalition Bid

Incumbent Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu announced a push for a broad national government on Monday, arguing that voters selected him to lead the next administration. Speaking through a statement on Likud's messaging channel, Netanyahu asserted that a clear majority of the Jewish public supported his Likud party as the largest political entity.

Netanyahu ruled out any partnership with Arab-majority lists. He alleged that these factions do not support the existence of the state and claimed they support terrorism. Leaders of the Arab-majority lists previously condemned the October 7, 2023, attack by Hamas without designating the group as a terrorist organization, and while the head of the Raam party has acknowledged Israel as a Jewish state, other leaders have rejected that definition.

## Path to Government Formation

President Isaac Herzog is set to begin formal consultations with party representatives on Wednesday. Under standard procedure, the president selects the candidate who receives the highest number of parliamentary recommendations, giving them 28 days to form a coalition, with a possible 14-day extension.

Internal divisions persist within the opposition regarding potential collaboration with Arab parties. While left-leaning leader Yair Golan has advocated for partnership, right-winger Avigdor Lieberman strongly opposes it, and Eisenkot's party has appeared to dismiss the notion without a direct statement.
