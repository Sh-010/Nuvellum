## Background on 'The Social Reckoning'

Screenwriter Aaron Sorkin has clarified that he was never asked to leave out references to Donald Trump in his upcoming film *The Social Reckoning*, premiering October 9. Sorkin told *Vanity Fair* that after he submitted his script, he met with Tom Rothman, who supported the project immediately.

Sorkin stated that he made an unprompted promise to exclude political signage, such as "Make America Great Again," from the film.

## Avoiding partisan framing

Although Facebook has played a major role in recent United States politics, Sorkin said he did not intend for the movie to serve as a political statement or "a red-blue thing," despite acknowledging that audiences might interpret it that way.

"The movie is about Facebook’s role and the rise of divisiveness, extremism, and hyper-partisanship in the country," Sorkin said.

## Cast and plot details

*The Social Reckoning* serves as a follow-up to the 2010 film *The Social Network*. Sorkin returned to write and direct the sequel, which centers on a 2021 document leak highlighting societal harm caused by the platform.

The cast features Mikey Madison as Facebook engineer Frances Haugen, who takes the company's internal secrets to *Wall Street Journal* reporter Jeff Horwitz, played by Jeremy Allen White. Jeremy Strong stars as Mark Zuckerberg, alongside cast members Bill Burr, Wunmi Mosaku, Billy Magnussen, and Betty Gilpin.
