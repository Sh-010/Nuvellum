During a panel discussion at MipJunior in Cannes, executives from Disney's Kids & Family division detailed the studio's strategy to address disruptions in youth viewing habits by partnering with prominent online creators such as Dhar Mann and Nastya.

## Meeting kids where they are

Ayo Davis, president of Disney’s Kids & Family division, pointed out that younger audiences are watching a wide variety of content across platforms. Davis emphasized that the studio needed to move quickly, rethink its approach to storytelling and franchises, and collaborate with industry partners to stay relevant while keeping audiences connected to the brand.

“You have to be willing to take chances, you have to be willing to expand, and you have to love what you do because you’re going to be pivoting on a regular basis in order to try to meet kids where they are,” Davis said.

## Collaborating with digital creators

Disney executives explained that working with creators is driven by where youth audiences engage daily, though they stressed that partnerships are formed with those who align with company values.

Dhar Mann's storytelling themes of good and evil fit with Disney's core narrative styles, according to Davis. Charlie Andrews, executive vice president of live action for Disney Kids & Family, noted that scripts for two greenlit series with Dhar Mann have been received. Meanwhile, Alyssa Sapire, head of original programming and strategy at Disney Jr., highlighted that Nastya connects well with preschoolers through imaginative play, while Andrews noted ongoing live-action development to keep pace with Nastya's demographic as she ages.

## Short-form content and audience research

Gino Guzzardo, vice president of multiplatform content, emphasized the role of short-form media, pointing to vertical short-film series like 'Locker Diaries' as a way to engage audiences across multiple screens. Additionally, Sapire noted that the preschool division regularly consults internal educational resource groups and conducts direct feedback sessions with children to guide writers and showrunners on story comprehension and development.
