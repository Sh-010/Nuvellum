## Hurricane Simon approaches the coast

Hurricane Simon moved parallel to Mexico's Pacific Coast on Sunday, lashing vulnerable resorts and towns with heavy rainfall and strong winds. Although the storm weakened to a category 3 system, the US National Hurricane Center stated that Simon is expected to remain a strong hurricane as it passes near west-central Mexico.

According to the hurricane center's advisory, Simon is anticipated to move farther offshore on Monday before turning back toward Mexico's coast on Wednesday.

## Alerts and emergency measures

Mexican authorities placed 28 municipalities across Jalisco and Colima states under the highest cyclone alert level, with an additional 93 municipalities under high alert. President Claudia Sheinbaum urged residents in coastal communities across Jalisco, Colima, Nayarit, and Michoacan to stay indoors.

Authorities established emergency shelters in threatened coastal areas, and Puerto Vallarta International Airport was temporarily closed while non-essential activities were suspended.

## Flooding and anticipated impacts

The storm's center approached the Puerto Vallarta area, where the most damaging winds and rains were anticipated around midnight through Monday morning. Senior hurricane center specialist Philippe Papin warned of catastrophic winds, life-threatening storm surges, and heavy rainfall likely to trigger dangerous flooding and mudslides in higher terrain areas between the western coast and the Sierra Madre mountains.

In Manzanillo, residents navigated flooded streets on Sunday morning as soldiers assisted with local rescues. The severe weather follows the recent impact of Hurricane Polo, which struck Mexico's Baja California Peninsula as a Category 2 storm, flooding streets and knocking down power lines.
