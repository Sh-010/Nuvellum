Following a White House ban on news organizations including CNN, MS NOW and Politico, a federal judge has extended a block preventing the administration from enforcing the restriction.

## Preliminary Injunction Issued

U.S. District Judge Timothy J. Kelly issued a ruling stating that the news outlets are likely to succeed on their due process claim as well as their two First Amendment claims. The Court granted a motion for a preliminary injunction, extending previous relief to preserve the status quo as the legal proceedings continue.

In the ruling, the judge noted that the decision does not suggest President Donald Trump’s concerns regarding media accuracy and fairness are unfounded, nor that the government lacks power when aiming to protect national security concerning the unlawful disclosure of classified information.

## Background of the Legal Dispute

The preliminary injunction follows an initial temporary restraining order issued by Judge Kelly on September 24. That order required the White House to immediately restore 'hard pass' press credentials for employees of CNN, MS NOW and Politico after they were revoked on or about September 18.

President Trump had announced on social media that he was banning the three outlets from the White House. When reporters attempted to enter the following day, they were denied entry despite holding valid credentials. In response, CNN, MS NOW and Politico filed a lawsuit against Trump and three White House officials, arguing that the ban violated the First Amendment and discriminated based on editorial viewpoint.
