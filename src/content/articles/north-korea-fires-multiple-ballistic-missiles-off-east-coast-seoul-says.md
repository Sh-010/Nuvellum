North Korea fired multiple ballistic missiles off its east coast on Monday, according to South Korea's military. The launches follow weeks of mounting friction between the two countries over a border landmine explosion that injured three South Korean soldiers last month.

## Missile launches and surveillance

South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff stated that several ballistic missiles were launched from the Pyongyang area toward the East Sea, also known as the Sea of Japan, at approximately 09:20 (00:20 GMT). In response to the launches, Seoul's military announced it has stepped up surveillance and vigilance for potential additional activity.

Japan's defence ministry separately confirmed the launch of at least one suspected ballistic missile.

## Border tensions and investigations

Tensions across the heavily fortified border have escalated since two landmines detonated in the Seoul-controlled area of the Demilitarised Zone (DMZ), injuring the three South Korean soldiers. A joint investigation conducted by Seoul and the United Nations concluded that North Korea had planted the landmines over a year ago. Pyongyang has denied involvement.

Following the incident, Seoul demanded an apology from North Korea. Kim Yo Jong, the sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, rejected the demand and dismissed Seoul's call for an apology as despicable, while also declining a planned transfer of medical equipment.

Both nations remain technically at war, as the Korean conflict concluded in 1953 with a truce rather than a formal peace treaty.
