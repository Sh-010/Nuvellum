The British Broadcasting Corporation is restructuring its operations by combining television and audio commissioning teams across specific genres, while simultaneously moving to eliminate approximately 100 positions within its content division.

## Commissioning Restructuring

According to an internal memo sent by BBC content chief Kate Phillips, the organization will integrate TV and audio commissioning for comedy, arts, and classical music starting in January 2027. Phillips stated that the goal is to foster collaboration and develop strong content for audiences across platforms. 

Under the new structure, comedy chief Chris Sussman will manage the genre across both television and radio, supported by Radio 4 commissioning editor Julia McKenzie. Arts and classical music TV head Suzy Klein will oversee audio and video commissioning for arts alongside Radio 4 commissioning editor Matthew Dodd, while BBC Radio 3 and BBC Proms controller Sam Jackson will direct classical music audio and video commissioning.

## Financial Savings and Job Reductions

The structural changes form part of a broader corporate initiative to achieve £500 million in savings. The content unit is tasked with contributing £100 million to this target through various measures, including a £80 million reduction in commissioning spend and the elimination of 100 to 150 hours of original television and 350 to 400 hours of audio by March 2028.

To manage the personnel reductions by the end of the current financial year, the broadcaster indicated it will focus primarily on voluntary redundancies and shutting down vacant posts to limit compulsory job losses. Previous announcements related to the cost-cutting measures also noted the upcoming closure of services including BBC Three, BBC Scotland, and Radio 4 Extra by the spring of next year.
