## Expansion beyond the capital

Jakarta World Cinema has ventured outside the Indonesian capital for the first time, establishing concurrent satellite events in Bandung and Malang. Both cities were chosen for their large student populations and active creative communities. The regional editions launched on Nov. 14 and are scheduled to run through Nov. 21, allowing audiences outside Jakarta to experience the event locally.

Screenings in Bandung are hosted at CGV Paris Van Java, while Malang's program takes place at Cinépolis Malang Town Square. Bandung Mayor Muhammad Farhan attended the opening night, noting that the event provides local audiences with access to alternative cinema outside of mainstream Hollywood releases.

## International film slate

The Bandung edition features a lineup of 203 international titles originating from 69 countries. The festival opened with Cristian Mungiu's "Fjord," which received this year's Palme d’Or at Cannes. Other highlighted international selections include Beth de Araujo’s "Josephine," Marine Atlan’s “La Gradiva,” Sandra Wollner’s “Everytime,” and Kurosawa Kiyoshi’s “The Samurai and the Prisoner.” 

Additionally, an online component running concurrently through Nov. 21 showcases works such as Ildiko Enyedi’s "Silent Friend" and Ilker Çatak’s "Yellow Letters."

## Closing events and local focus

Executive director Frederica stated that organizers had observed strong demand from movie enthusiasts outside Jakarta for several years, making the regional expansion a natural progression. 

Unlike the Jakarta event, the Bandung edition will conclude with a free open-air screening for the general public on Nov. 21. This outdoor program will highlight productions by local university students and winners of the KlikFilm Short Movie Competition, concluding with a restored screening of Usmar Ismail’s 1954 Indonesian classic, "Lewat Djam Malam" ("After the Curfew").
