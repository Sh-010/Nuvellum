Aston Martin competitor Fernando Alonso has reiterated his stance regarding the standard of driving in the lower tier of Formula 1. The remarks follow a first-lap collision involving Alonso and Williams driver Alex Albon during the United States Grand Prix in Austin.

## Austin Collision and Subsequent Criticisms

During the opening lap at the Austin circuit, Albon attempted an overtake on Alonso at the Turn 11 hairpin. The maneuver resulted in contact that damaged both vehicles. Neither driver incurred a penalty from the incident, and both finished the race, with Alonso taking 16th place and Albon 17th.

Following the event, Alonso expressed frustration with competitors in the bottom third of the grid, describing the driving quality as low and pointing to frequent opening-lap incidents. Speaking ahead of the Mexico City Grand Prix, Alonso stated that repeated mistakes and early race incidents would displease him if he were a team principal. He contrasted the performance with drivers who overcome grid penalties to win races, suggesting that confidence and patience are necessary during the opening lap.

## Responses From the Grid

Albon addressed the remarks during an official press conference, stating he disagreed with the characterization of the field. The Williams driver expressed disappointment at hearing the comments from someone he looked up to during his youth, while maintaining respect for his colleague. Albon argued that the current Formula 1 grid is stronger than in previous eras and noted the absence of traditional pay drivers.

Alonso further contended that driving near the back presents distinct challenges due to cars having less grip and difficult handling characteristics. However, he argued that unnecessary contact creates avoidable problems, particularly under financial regulations like the cost cap. Alonso added that he does not plan to raise the topic during the drivers' meeting, noting that it forms part of competition and that his own team must qualify higher on the grid to avoid those positions.
