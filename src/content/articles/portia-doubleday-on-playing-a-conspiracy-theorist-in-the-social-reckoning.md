## Bringing real-world radicalization to the screen

In Aaron Sorkin's film *The Social Reckoning*, actress Portia Doubleday portrays Kaylee, a character whose descent into online extremism serves as the emotional catalyst for the movie. In a new interview, Doubleday discussed how she drew on personal experiences when approaching the role, noting that she had known people in her own life who became radicalized.

"I actually knew a couple people that were in the pre-med track and who were radicalized," Doubleday told *Variety*. She recalled questioning how individuals trained in scientific methodology could end up believing in fringe conspiracies. "Repeated exposure can start to make extreme ideas feel normal," she said, describing how echo chambers and frequent exposure to basement-produced videos can acclimate people to alternate narratives.

## Grounding a complex character

In the film's opening scene, Kaylee is found by police by her stalled car, visibly impaired and glued to her phone. Rather than cooperating, she escalates the situation by shouting insults and broadcasting the interaction online under the "Voice of Reason" channel. Later in the film, the character's trajectory intersects with Facebook whistleblower Frances Haugen (played by Mikey Madison), eventually leading to Kaylee participating in the January 6 insurrection.

Doubleday emphasized that Sorkin wanted the performance to remain grounded rather than reducing the character's behavior simply to being intoxicated. The actress collaborated with the director to ensure the portrayal captured the emotional addiction and feelings of victimization that pull individuals toward extremist groups.

## Tackling heavy subject matter

Filming the pivotal opening sequence presented unique challenges, including delivering Sorkin's rhythmic dialogue while slurring words. Doubleday credited the writer-director's script for providing stability during the intense two-day shoot.

Reflecting on shooting the scenes depicting the January 6 Capitol attack, Doubleday expressed sadness at inhabiting the mindset of someone caught up in such events. "Behind amplifying extremism and misinformation and Facebook's hand in this: What is this doing to one person that ends up being there?" she said. *The Social Reckoning* is currently playing in theaters.
