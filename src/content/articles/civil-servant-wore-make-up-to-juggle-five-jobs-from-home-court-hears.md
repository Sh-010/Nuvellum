## Court trial opens at Southwark Crown Court

A 55-year-old civil servant from east London is on trial accused of nine counts of fraud, which he denies. Kashim Chowdhury is accused of earning nearly £350,000 while allegedly juggling five full-time public sector roles simultaneously between February 2020 and September 2023. Prosecutors allege he used sick notes and worked from home to manage the workload.

Mr Chowdhury denies acting dishonestly or intending to make a dishonest gain. The prosecution alleges he secured the positions by making "false representations" about his employment status during the application process. 

## Overlapping public sector roles

Over a three-year period, the defendant allegedly held concurrent positions across multiple government departments and local authorities. These included the Home Office, the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs (Defra), the UK Health Security Agency, the Department of Health and Social Care, and Tower Hamlets Council in east London.

According to the prosecution, Mr Chowdhury "exploited" the remote working arrangements established during the Covid-19 pandemic. He reportedly informed employers that he needed to work from home to care for an unwell mother. 

## Video call allegation

Jurors at Southwark Crown Court heard details of a video call held in 2023 between the defendant and his manager at the Health Security Agency, Aleesha Adams, to discuss his sick leave. 

Prosecutor Lucy Organ told the court that Mr Chowdhury wore white make-up during the meeting to create the appearance of illness. Ms Adams claimed the make-up began to separate on his forehead, revealing his real skin underneath.

"There could be no doubt about it, the defendant had applied make up to his face for this Teams meeting to try and pretend to his line manager that he was unwell," Ms Organ told the jury, describing the incident as demonstrating the lengths of the alleged deceit.

The court heard that the workload eventually became unmanageable and his performance declined. While he was signed off from some positions due to mixed anxiety and a depressive disorder, prosecutors stated he did not submit sick notes to all of his employers. The alleged activity was uncovered after the National Fraud Initiative alerted Defra. 

The trial continues.
