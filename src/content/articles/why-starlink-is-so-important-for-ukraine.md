Few technological systems have played as vital a role in the war in Ukraine as Starlink, which serves as a cornerstone for the communications, reconnaissance, and drone operations of the Ukrainian armed forces. Recent reports of disruptions and restrictions have raised widespread concerns in Kyiv about the country's heavy reliance on the satellite internet service.

## Communications and the battlefield

Since Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022, Ukraine has extensively used the satellite internet service operated by US company SpaceX. The technology allows military personnel to maintain communications and data exchange even when local infrastructure is destroyed or cellphone networks fail. According to the Center for Security Studies (CSS) at ETH Zurich, approximately 50,000 Starlink terminals—specialized receiving and transmitting devices—were in use in Ukraine in 2025 to coordinate operations and relay command orders.

Starlink emerged as a crucial alternative early in the conflict after a cyberattack targeted the KA-SAT satellite system favored by Ukraine just hours before the invasion. While Starlink is a commercial service, SpaceX also offers Starshield, a variant specifically designed for governmental and military applications. Elon Musk recently stated that Starshield systems are controlled by the US government, whereas Starlink is operated commercially by SpaceX. Recent restriction reports primarily affect systems used for military purposes.

## The impact on drone warfare

Beyond basic communications, Starlink is heavily integrated into Ukraine's drone operations. The system provides real-time internet connections for ground, naval, and aerial drones, allowing operators to transmit video footage and control systems over long distances for reconnaissance and deep-strike missions. 

Ukrainian media reported that SpaceX temporarily introduced speed restrictions for Starlink terminals in motion, limiting them to about 160 kilometers per hour (100 mph). Such constraints directly affect fast-moving Ukrainian drones that travel up to 300 kilometers per hour (186 mph), cutting off live video feeds and real-time control if connections are lost.

## Strategic dependence

While Starlink and Starshield are essential to Ukraine's defense, they operate alongside other assets, including western intelligence and reconnaissance data regarding air attacks, missile launches, and troop movements. Although European alternatives such as Eutelsat/OneWeb exist, their capacity to match Starlink's wartime performance remains untested on a large scale. Experts note that Ukraine's reliance on a private technology provider highlights a fundamental shift in modern warfare.
