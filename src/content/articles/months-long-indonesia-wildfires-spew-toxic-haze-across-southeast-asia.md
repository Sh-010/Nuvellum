Parts of southern Southeast Asia have been placed under the highest-level haze alert as wildfires that have swept Indonesia for months continue to emit toxic smoke across the region. Meteorological monitors and officials note that seasonal wildfires are more severe this year due to drought conditions driven by a "super-El Nino" effect.

## Highest-level regional alert

The ASEAN Specialised Meteorological Centre (ASMC) activated Alert Level 3 for the Southern ASEAN region, representing the highest rung of a four-tiered alert system. The designation indicates a high risk of severe transboundary haze characterized by widespread moderate to dense smoke observed over consecutive days. 

The prolonged pollution has restricted outdoor activities, shuttered schools, and caused a rise in respiratory conditions across Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, and East Timor. Monitoring platforms ranked Singapore and Kuala Lumpur among the world's most polluted major cities with very hazardous air quality levels.

## Environmental impact and local response

Foresighted peatland and forest fires—most notably on Sumatra and Borneo—have burned unchecked during the dry season, which typically runs from April to October. Environmental group Auriga reported that more than 637,000 hectares burned between January and August, while deforestation monitor Nusantara Atlas estimated the affected area at nearly 900,000 hectares.

In Indonesia, authorities deployed planes to disperse 2.4 tonnes of dry ice over South Sumatra province's main airport in an attempt to reduce visibility-impairing haze. Meanwhile, Singapore announced plans to issue warnings to seven Indonesian companies identified as responsible for the blazes. Officials from the Philippines stated that the transboundary haze will be a topic of discussion at upcoming meetings ahead of the November ASEAN summit in Manila.
