Looters on Saturday seized the opportunity to raid and vandalise a host of shops and businesses across France—ranging from bakeries and supermarkets to technology stores—as hundreds of thousands of people took to the streets to protest higher living costs and other grievances.

## Incidents in Paris and Marseille

According to witnesses and authorities, looters in several French cities broke into stores on the sidelines of cost-of-living demonstrations. Near the République square in Paris, the front window of a skincare brand collapsed as beauty products were tossed to crowds outside, and a telecommunications shop was broken into. The main monument in République was covered in graffiti with paving stones ripped up, while a bar named “La Barrique” caught fire. Youths also broke into a nearby supermarket to grab alcohol and drink cans to throw at police officers.

In Marseille, France's second-largest city, protesters vandalised dozens of shops, targeting luxury brand Hermès alongside stores selling jewellery, clothes, makeup, household appliances, and bread. The fire brigade intervened to extinguish a fire at a shoe shop.

## Unrest in Regional Cities

In Lille, Socialist mayor Arnaud Deslandes reported that shops were vandalised, including an Apple Store in the city centre. Several stores were also looted in Nice, taking in sportswear outlets and a motorbike dealership.

Public anger has mounted in recent weeks amid dwindling purchasing power since 2024 and soaring fuel prices following the start of the US-Iran war earlier in the year. The demonstrations come as France prepares for elections next year to replace President Emmanuel Macron, alongside growing public frustration over perceived police brutality during high-school student protests.
