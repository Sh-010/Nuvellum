Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy appeared at the European Union summit in Brussels, where EU leaders reaffirmed their support for his country. However, Ukrainian leadership faces a difficult winter amid a public dispute with US President Donald Trump, concerns over US relations with Russia, satellite internet disruptions, and substantial budget requirements.

## US Relations and Financial Pressures

Zelenskyy's visit to Brussels coincides with strained relations between Kyiv and Washington. US President Donald Trump stated in an interview with Newsmax that he finds Zelenskyy more difficult to deal with than Russian President Vladimir Putin. Furthermore, a recent deal by the US to purchase Russian diesel marked a step back from sanctions, which was described as a blow to Ukraine. Following Ukrainian strikes on Russian oil refineries, Trump labeled the decision a disgrace.

At the summit press conference, Zelenskyy noted that US-built missiles are being sent to the Middle East, indicating Ukraine is not currently a priority. Consequently, Kyiv is turning to Europe for military power. Olena Prokopenko, a senior fellow at the German Marshall Fund of the United States, told DW that Ukraine is entering arguably its most difficult winter since the full-scale invasion.

Zelenskyy also urged EU leaders to provide nearly $80 billion (€70 billion) in financial backing next year to help hold Russia back. Prokopenko noted that the war is becoming objectively more expensive while Russian strikes reduce Ukrainian tax and customs revenues. European Council President Antonio Costa stated that Ukraine remains a priority and asked the European Commission to propose a funding plan for 2027, while European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen promised the EU will continue to lead.

## Starlink Disclosures and Technical Challenges

Another focal point at the summit was disruptions to Starlink, the satellite internet service developed by Elon Musk's SpaceX. Ukraine utilizes both standard Starlink and Starshield, its military-grade version, for battlefield communications, mid-range strikes, and interceptor drones.

Zelenskyy expressed deep and unpleasant surprise over access limitations that he said hobbled Ukraine's defense capabilities. Both the White House and Elon Musk stated they were not responsible for the slowdown. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas noted that Starlink is necessary for Ukraine and that Europe cannot replace the capability in the short term. Zelenskyy reported that Starlink speeds have improved but not fully restored, and Ukraine is working with countries including France to find a partial solution.
