A delegation of European diplomats and officials has been attacked by masked Israeli settlers in the occupied West Bank. The incident occurred in the Christian village of Taybeh near Ramallah, where the envoys from Belgium, France, Poland, and the Catholic Church had gathered to assess conditions on the ground during the annual olive harvest following reports of frequent settler attacks.

Video footage from the scene captured masked individuals confronting the diplomatic convoy, with reports indicating that stones were thrown and a consular vehicle's window was smashed. Bernard Baudoux, the mayor of Aulnoye-Aymeries in northern France who was among the visitors, stated that he was kicked, beaten with iron bars, and thrown to the ground during the assault.

## International and diplomatic reactions

European nations issued sharp condemnations of the violence. Belgian Foreign Minister Maxime Prevot stated that Israel bears the responsibility to bring those responsible to justice and to ensure the safety of diplomats, journalists, and Palestinians. Prevot added that Belgium will continue pushing for European Union sanctions against perpetrators of the violence.

French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot described the assault as "appalling" and called on Israeli authorities to halt criminal violence and guarantee civilian safety. Poland's foreign affairs ministry similarly demanded a firm response from Israeli authorities to end the cycle of violence in the region. The European Union urged adherence to international and human rights law, calling for full accountability and effective measures to stop ongoing attacks against Palestinians and journalists.

The incident followed a separate assault a day prior in another West Bank village, where Palestinians and Washington Post photographer Heidi Levine were injured. That attack drew condemnation from United States officials, including US Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee, who called it a "despicable act of terror carried out by masked cowards," and US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who described it as disturbing.

## Official responses from Israel

Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar strongly condemned the violence by what he termed "Israeli rioters" and noted that the foreign ministry was in communication with the ambassadors of the affected countries. Sa'ar added that the visit had not been coordinated with the Israel Defense Forces in advance, stating that prior coordination could have assisted in securing the visitors.

The Israel Defense Forces also condemned the severe act of violence and stated that a preliminary inquiry indicated no soldiers were present at the scene. 

Meanwhile, the office of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu urged caution before drawing conclusions, suggesting the reports were part of a coordinated campaign to smear Israel and Jewish residents during the olive harvest. Netanyahu's office stated that police and forensic investigators are examining the circumstances and identifying those responsible.
