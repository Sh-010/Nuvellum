## The mystery of the full name

During Scotland's match against Slovenia at the Stozice Arena, attention was drawn to an unusual feature on the pitch. While footballers typically display only their surnames, Robbie Ure stepped out wearing both his first and last names on the back of his number 10 shirt. 

The full name sparked curiosity among observers during the first half, leading to questions after the match about the reasoning behind the choice.

## Robbie Ure's explanation

Speaking to BBC Sport Scotland, the 22-year-old Sevilla striker clarified that people frequently refer to him by his full name rather than his surname alone. Additionally, he noted a personal aesthetic preference regarding the length of his last name.

"A lot of people will just call me, they'll always say Robbie Ure," he explained. "They wouldn't just say Ure, so that was a reason. And then, to be honest, I think U-R-E is just so small, I just don't really like it. So, I put my first name in as well. I've got it at club level too, I just prefer it."

## Debut under Sebastien Pocognoli

The fixture marked the first line-up under new manager Sebastien Pocognoli, with Ure earning his international debut alongside fellow newcomer Stephen Welsh. Ure featured alongside Oli McBurnie in a bold formation before being replaced by Kieron Bowie in the 67th minute.

Reflecting on his first appearance for his country, the former Rangers youth player expressed pride despite a scoreless first half and a final draw in the Nations League opener.

"I loved every minute of it," Ure said. "Obviously, disappointed not to win the game. With the second half performance we probably deserved to but making my debut for Scotland, it's an amazing feeling and I'm really proud and hungry for more."
