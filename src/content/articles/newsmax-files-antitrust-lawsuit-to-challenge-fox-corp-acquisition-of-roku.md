## Antitrust challenge filed over streaming market share

Newsmax initiated legal action on Thursday in the Northern District of California to block Fox Corp.’s $22 billion acquisition of Roku. The network argues the transaction grants Fox unlawful monopoly control over the free streaming market. The Department of Justice previously cleared the transaction earlier in the month after securing commitments from Fox to maintain Roku as an open and neutral platform.

Despite regulatory clearance, Newsmax contends that these assurances are unenforceable and expresses concern over losing access to a vital distribution channel. In a public statement, Newsmax CEO Christopher Ruddy stated that no individual media organization should possess the authority to dictate which news outlets reach the public.

## Market dominance and platform distribution concerns

The legal filing asserts that Fox will command approximately 70 percent of the free, ad-supported streaming television sector through its combined ownership of Roku, the Roku Channel, and Tubi. The complaint points to past actions, noting that Fox previously declined to host Newsmax on Tubi, which raises apprehension that the company might restrict distribution on Roku as well.

Fox Corp. defended the acquisition, stating that the transaction underwent thorough regulatory scrutiny and characterizing the legal challenge as entirely without merit. A spokesperson added that integration efforts are underway to benefit consumers and industry partners.

## Ongoing legal disputes between networks

This legal filing adds to prior litigation between the two media competitors. Newsmax previously brought a separate antitrust case in federal court alleging that Fox utilized aggressive negotiating strategies to restrict broader cable and satellite availability. That prior litigation experienced procedural hurdles involving venue transfers and pending motions to dismiss.
