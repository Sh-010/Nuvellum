Filmmakers Joel and Ethan Coen received the Lumière Award at the Lumière Film Festival in Lyon, marking four decades of cinematic collaboration. The ceremony opened to the Gipsy Kings' Spanish-language rendition of “Hotel California,” a nod to the directors' comedy “The Big Lebowski.”

Accepting the honor, Joel Coen reflected on their career in storytelling. "We’ve had, pretty much with certain exceptions, nothing but fun making these films over the decades," he said. Ethan Coen joked during the speeches, thanking the Lumières for inventing the movie camera.

## Star-Studded Tribute

The gathering included actors Javier Bardem and Frances McDormand, alongside directors Wim Wenders, Robert Eggers, Michel Hazanavicius, and other figures from the film industry. Bardem delivered a tribute sharing memories from making "No Country for Old Men," recalling how he initially questioned his fit for the project due to his aversion to violence, lack of driving skills, and limited English.

"They gave me no reason whatsoever to do the movie. They were just laughing," Bardem said of the directors. He ultimately praised the experience, describing the role as "one masterpiece and the gift of a lifetime, personally and professionally."

## Festival Tradition and Film History

Festival director Thierry Frémaux presented the Coen brothers with photographs matching their poses to those of the Lumière brothers. In keeping with festival tradition, the filmmakers were tasked with shooting their own version of the Lumières' early film “Workers Leaving the Lumière Factory” before the event concluded.

The evening also incorporated musical performances, with Bénabar performing Charles Aznavour’s “Parce que tu crois” and Angélique Kidjo singing Jacques Brel’s “Ne me quitte pas.” Screenings included Ettore Scola’s short film “1943-1997,” prompting Bardem to comment on the enduring relevance of cinema's power to provide refuge.

The Lumière Film Festival runs through Sunday, Oct. 18.
