Ahead of the United States Grand Prix in Austin, Formula 1 driver Max Verstappen has voiced strong concerns regarding the state of the track surface at Circuit of The Americas, describing the bumps as out of control and unacceptable.

## Spine Health and Track Concerns

Speaking to media outlets, Verstappen stated that medical checks on the lower part of his spine revealed damage. He noted that driving on rough tracks contributes to physical strain, adding that previous car setups over recent years have compounded the issue. The Red Bull driver indicated that organisers should completely open up the track to properly address the foundation issues rather than relying on temporary grinding.

Despite praising the venue's layout and city location, Verstappen suggested that soil issues should have been anticipated with better structural reinforcement.

## Driver Reactions on Circuit Conditions

Other drivers also commented on the challenges posed by the circuit's rough tarmac. Reigning world champion Lando Norris mentioned that he consumes headache tablets prior to driving at certain venues due to severe bouncing against the headrests. Norris added that the circuit would fail to meet current regulatory tolerances if it were newly constructed.

Mercedes driver George Russell offered a different perspective, characterising the bumps as a standard racing condition while suggesting that circuits generally require more frequent repaving. Russell pointed out that modern Formula 1 cars operate with minimal ground clearance and lack the tyre suspension compliance of previous eras, intensifying the impact of track irregularities.
