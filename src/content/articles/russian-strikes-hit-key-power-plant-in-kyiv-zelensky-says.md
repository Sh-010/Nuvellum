A key energy facility supplying heating and electricity to Kyiv has been struck in a Russian ballistic missile attack, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said.

## Strikes on Kyiv and casualties

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky stated that the Darnytska CHP-4 facility and a residential building were hit in the attack, which he said occurred without early warning from partners. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko reported that two people were killed and 21 others were injured in the missile strike on the apartment block late on Tuesday. Klitschko added that several energy facilities in the capital had been hit.

The massive missile attacks knocked out power to parts of Kyiv in the early hours of Wednesday, prompting city officials to declare a red alert due to a missile threat and urge residents to remain in shelters. It marks the second consecutive night that Kyiv has been targeted by Russian missiles.

## Reactions and ceasefire context

President Zelensky criticized the strikes, noting that Ukraine had supported an energy ceasefire while Russia targeted power infrastructure and civilian lives. He remarked that it was unjust to face infrastructure destruction without early ballistic missile warnings and called for international awareness, particularly urging the United States to observe the ongoing situation.

The strikes followed US President Donald Trump's announcement two days prior that an energy ceasefire had been agreed upon by Russia and Ukraine. When asked if Russia had broken the ceasefire, Trump demurred, stating that both countries want the war to end but do not know how to achieve it. Trump also denied speculation that America had cut off access to intelligence tracking incoming threats, affirming that the US is sharing intelligence and working with both nations.

Previously, Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov stated that Ukraine does not deserve the respite of a ceasefire, arguing that Europe would use any pause to supply new weapons for the continuation of the conflict.

## History of Darnytska CHP-4

The Darnytska CHP-4 facility, which supplies heating and electricity to the Dniprovskyi district and parts of the Darnytskyi district, has experienced multiple strikes during the war. A separate Russian missile attack in February severely damaged the plant and forced a shutdown, leaving over 1,000 residential buildings, along with schools, kindergartens, and medical institutions, without heating and hot water.
