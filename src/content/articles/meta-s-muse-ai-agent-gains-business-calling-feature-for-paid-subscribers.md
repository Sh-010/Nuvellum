Meta has expanded the capabilities of its consumer artificial intelligence agent, Muse, allowing it to place phone calls to domestic businesses on behalf of users. The functionality, which follows an initial testing period, is restricted to paying customers.

## Subscription Tiers and Use Cases

According to the company, Muse has surpassed 10 million users as it attempts to make consumer AI mainstream by handling routine tasks. Alexandr Wang, Meta's Chief AI officer and head of Superintelligence Labs, stated that the tool can manage calls that individuals prefer to avoid, such as contacting cable providers, government motor vehicle departments, or restaurants lacking online reservation systems.

Access to the feature requires a paid subscription. Plans begin at $20 per month for 500 million weekly tokens, while heavier users can select a $100 monthly tier providing 3 billion tokens. In addition to calling, Wang noted that a recently introduced option to create a Muse email address will soon become available to free users.

## Industry Context and Challenges

Muse enters a competitive landscape where other systems, including Google's Gemini and a tool from startup Instinct, already offer business-calling capabilities. However, adoption faces practical hurdles on the receiving end. Many business receptionists disconnect immediately upon realizing they are speaking with an artificial agent. 

Despite mixed reception and potential difficulties in telling artificial and human callers apart as voice synthesis improves, developers view the feature as a potential relief for consumers weary of navigating phone trees and waiting on hold.
