## Venture Capital Returns and Unusual Stake Sizes

When artificial intelligence company Anthropic goes public, several venture capital firms expect to secure billions in returns. According to Anthropic's S-1 filing, prominent investors such as Menlo Ventures, Sequoia, Sound Ventures, and Spark Capital hold positions that will return substantial sums. However, none of these venture firms own 5% of the company, which is the threshold required for inclusion in the principal stockholders' table. In typical technology startup public offerings, lead venture investors usually maintain double-digit percentage stakes.

The absence of large percentage stakes stems from Anthropic's immense valuation. At a private valuation of $965 billion, a 1% stake equals roughly $10 billion. Consequently, Menlo's position of approximately $14 billion represents only about 1.5% of the company.

## Strategic Backers and Convertible Notes

While venture firms will see large payouts, strategic business partners are positioned to capture even greater financial benefits. Amazon represents the single largest financial beneficiary of the public offering. Beginning in 2023, Amazon structured much of its early investment through convertible notes, which are debt instruments that convert into shares during an IPO or sale. 

Data from the S-1 filing indicates that Amazon held convertible notes with $6.2 billion in principal outstanding as of June 30. As Anthropic's valuation increased across funding rounds, the value of those underlying notes surged. Amazon's filings valued the note holdings at $97.9 billion as of June 30, and these notes could convert into as many as 1.84 billion shares. A cap limits Amazon to 12% of the partially diluted share count, though the company holds the option to waive this restriction.

In addition to the debt instruments, Amazon invested $10 billion in nonvoting preferred stock during the second quarter by contributing $5 billion to each of Anthropic's last two funding rounds. Combined with earlier backing, Amazon's total investment reaches approximately $18 billion. Furthermore, Anthropic committed to spending at least $110 billion on Amazon Web Services over the next decade.

## Google's Position and Future Warnings

Google is also positioned for a major financial windfall through the listing and separate cloud computing agreements. Court filings and analyst estimates indicate Google holds a stake of roughly 5.6%, alongside securing at least $114 billion in cloud commitments from Anthropic through 2033. Despite the lucrative outlook, the S-1 filing cautions that some cloud providers also develop competing artificial intelligence models and may maintain diverging strategic priorities.
