## Rising costs and rural exposure

Residents in the South West of England report struggling to heat their homes amid surging heating oil costs driven by the conflict involving the US, Israel, and Iran. According to Minister for Energy Consumers Polly Billington, heating fuel costs have increased by up to 82% since the start of the conflict.

Billington stated that rural communities with many off-grid residents are among the most exposed to the price increases, adding that officials need to ensure the market functions as fairly as possible.

## Consumer impact and budgeting

Some residents have delayed purchasing fuel for the winter in the hope that prices might decrease, while others fear people may go without heat. Nicola Sharpe from Dunsford, Devon, stated that 500 litres of heating oil cost about £340 at this time last year, compared to just over £600 currently. She noted she is looking for ways to spread the cost.

Pauline Trubody, a 76-year-old resident from Porkellis, Cornwall, reported spending more on two deliveries of 500 litres this year than previously and indicated she would likely reduce heating usage during the day.

Lee Fossey from St Agnes, Cornwall, stated that despite budgeting £30 a month into a savings scheme, the dramatic price increases mean she can no longer afford to fill her tank, leaving her facing the prospect of having no heating or seeking an alternative source.

## Government response and measures

In October, the government announced plans to regulate the heating oil sector by tightening rules, promoting competition, and offering clearer compensation routes. Ministers also committed to reviewing minimum order levels set for certain customers.

However, some consumers expressed skepticism regarding the impact of these measures. Additionally, the government previously announced a £53m support package in March aimed at low-income families utilizing heating oil.
