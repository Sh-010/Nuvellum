## A hub for political action

As the November 3 midterm elections approach, Manuel’s Tavern in Atlanta has once again become a gathering place for political observers. Patrons recently filled the establishment to watch the second and final debate between incumbent Democratic Senator Jon Ossoff and his Republican challenger, Mike Collins. 

While the crowd watched impassively, the debate touched on national themes as Collins opened by targeting Ossoff's reported presidential ambitions. Ossoff, the sole Democratic senator in the chamber seeking re-election in a state won by Donald Trump in 2024, maintained that he plans to keep his opponent on the defensive.

## Generations of history and politics

Established in 1956 by Manuel Maloof, the tavern has spent six decades hosting Democratic heavyweights and serving as a campaign launchpad. In 1970, former US President Jimmy Carter chose the venue to launch his gubernatorial campaign, initiating a close friendship with the founder.

Over the decades, the walls of Manuel’s Tavern have accumulated a collection of photographs and memorabilia documenting visits from prominent figures including Bill and Hillary Clinton, Al Gore, John Kerry, and Barack Obama, who famously played darts at the bar while waiting for an interview.

Manoel Maloof's involvement in local politics also helped shape the region. According to a local obituary, he played a central role in transforming DeKalb County from a suburban Republican area into an urbanised Democratic stronghold.

## An immigrant family legacy

The tavern's roots extend back to the founder's father, Gibran Maloof, who immigrated to the United States from northern Lebanon after World War I. Operating an oxcart to deliver mining supplies between Atlanta and Tennessee, he later purchased the Tip Top Billiard Parlor near the Georgia State Capitol.

Today, current owner Brian Maloof views his role as maintaining a historical institution that balances modern demands with decades of tradition. Following his death in 2004, Manuel Maloof's ashes were placed in an urn housed above the bar, where staff note he continues to watch over the establishment as patrons discuss politics by the windows.
