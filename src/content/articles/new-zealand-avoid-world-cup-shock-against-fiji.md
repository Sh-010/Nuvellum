## A hard-fought opening victory

New Zealand's women's rugby league team were forced to come from behind to secure an 18-12 victory against tournament debutants Fiji in their opening World Cup fixture in Newcastle.

As joint-most successful team in the history of the tournament with three titles, New Zealand entered the match as heavy favourites. However, they found themselves trailing 12-4 in the first half and 12-10 at the break, marking the first time the Ferns have ever been behind at half-time in a World Cup match.

Stacey Waaka opened the scoring for New Zealand after just two minutes, but Fiji responded strongly. Skipper Sereana Naitokatoka set up Losana Lutu for Fiji's first-ever women's World Cup try in the 21st minute, before Sienna Laing extended the debutants' lead five minutes later.

## Overcoming the deficit

Waaka scored her second try before the interval to narrow the gap, but Fiji's defence maintained pressure through the second half. New Zealand eventually drew level and took the lead through penalty kicks by Zehara Temara, before sealing the result in the final minutes when a cross-field kick from Raecene McGregor allowed Leianne Tufuga to score.

New Zealand coach Ricky Henry described the performance as a "wake-up call," noting that the team moved away from their intended game plan. Player Georgia Hale acknowledged that the side played with uncharacteristic ill-discipline and untidy positioning.

Following the fixture, New Zealand are scheduled to face France in Christchurch in Group B, while Fiji's next match is against Papua New Guinea.
