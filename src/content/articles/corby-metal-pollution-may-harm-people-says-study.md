## Citizen science report highlights metal levels

Campaigners examining the legacy of the historic toxic waste scandal in Corby report finding metal and metalloid presences that could present risks to human health. Charity Earthwatch Europe states that trained citizen scientists identified potentially harmful levels of metals across 52 freshwater sites and 28 soil locations in the Northamptonshire town.

According to the charity, every tested freshwater site contained at least one metal or metalloid exceeding ecological reference concentrations. Nickel was detected in 28 freshwater samples, and cadmium was found in six. Earthwatch Europe noted that elevated concentrations of cadmium and nickel in freshwater, as well as cadmium and arsenic in soil, were largely concentrated near Deene Quarry and a former slag heap in Weldon.

Dr Sasha Woods, a scientific advisor for the group, stated that the results highlight locations requiring further investigation and remedial action. She indicated that findings show certain metals present in freshwater at levels capable of affecting aquatic life, and in soil at levels that could impact human health, especially near historic waste sites. The organization suggested that the data points to a relatively constant pollution source, such as groundwater and landfill leachate, rather than urban run-off.

## Official responses and community reaction

The Environment Agency (EA) welcomed the work and stated it is reviewing the report alongside its own data. However, a spokesperson maintained that monitoring to date does not indicate "widespread or significant harm to the freshwater environment across the Corby area," noting that some metals occur naturally.

Tracey Taylor, a local campaigner whose baby daughter died at four days old following the historical steelworks demolition scandal in the 1980s, expressed hope that the new findings would prompt action. "They should not have to deal with this or be living with this on their land. It needs sorting," she said.

Lee Barron, the Member of Parliament for Corby and East Northamptonshire, stated that local authorities have a duty to inspect sites where contamination creates unacceptable risks. He called on North Northamptonshire Council to publish a plan to secure the sites. A council spokesperson welcomed the report, assuring residents that credible evidence regarding risks to human health or the environment will be thoroughly reviewed through regulatory processes.
