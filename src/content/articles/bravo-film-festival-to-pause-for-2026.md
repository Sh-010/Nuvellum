Organizers behind the Bravo Film Festival have announced that the 2026 edition of the event will not take place. According to festival leadership, the cancellation is due to circumstances beyond their control, though preparation for the following year's edition remains underway.

## Commitment to Brazilian cinema

Talize Sayegh, the festival's founder and executive director, stated that the organization's dedication to Brazilian filmmakers, partners, and audiences persists. 

"Our commitment to Brazilian cinema and to the filmmakers, partners, and audiences who have supported Bravo has not changed," Sayegh said in a statement. "We are grateful for that community and focused on returning next year with a festival worthy of it."

## Festival background and partnerships

Founded in 2008, the Los Angeles-based event operates across November and frequently functions as a United States showcase for Brazilian talent. The festival traditionally opens by screening Brazil's submission for the Best International Oscar, which included Walter Salles' *I’m Still Here* in 2024 and *The Secret Agent*, directed by Kleber Mendonça Filho and starring Wagner Moura, in 2025. Past editions have opened at venues such as the Academy Museum.

The organization maintains longstanding partnerships with the American Cinematheque, the Brazilian Consulate in Los Angeles, Cinema do Brasil, and Projeto Paradiso.

Thiago Macêdo Correia, the creative director of the festival, noted that the pause provides time to continue developing ties between Brazilian filmmakers and Hollywood ahead of a planned return.

"The work continues, and we are excited to return next year with the stories, talent, and energy that define Brazilian cinema," Correia said.
