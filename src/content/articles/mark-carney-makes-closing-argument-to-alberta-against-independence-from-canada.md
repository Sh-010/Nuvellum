Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney made a final appeal to voters in Alberta considering a path toward independence, warning that a referendum is a dangerous gamble amid growing threats to Canada's sovereignty.

Speaking at a pro-Canada rally in Edmonton, Carney cautioned that the vote arrives at a critical juncture while U.S. President Donald Trump wages a trade war and makes threats regarding Canada. 

“This is not a game. This is not poker. A vote against Canada is a dangerous bluff that will lead to years of deep uncertainty and lost opportunities for Albertans and Canadians,” Carney told the crowd.

Monday’s vote does not directly decide independence. Instead, Alberta—which holds the fourth-largest proven oil reserves in the world—will decide whether to remain a province or instruct the government to begin the legal process toward a future separation referendum.

## Political unity and division

Carney’s closing appeal followed two days of campaigning against separation in the province. Earlier on Friday, the prime minister appeared alongside Alberta’s conservative premier, Danielle Smith, in Red Deer in a show of unity against independence. Smith praised Carney for improving relations with a province that frequently clashed with his predecessor, Justin Trudeau, over carbon taxes and other energy policies.

Smith called the referendum despite opposing independence herself, citing a poll showing 22% support for separation, and faces pressure from separatists within her governing United Conservative Party. Former Premier Jason Kenney attributed the growth of the independence movement within the party to activists angered by COVID-19 restrictions.

While Smith and Carney united against separation, they remain divided on nine other questions on Monday’s ballot concerning immigration and provincial powers. Smith urged voters to approve them, while Carney urged their rejection.

## External and internal pressures

During his rallies, Carney pointed to external pressures, warning that outsiders are working to break the country. The crowd responded with loud applause when he spoke about reducing Canada's economic dependence on the United States.

Federal Conservative leader Pierre Poilievre also urged Albertans to remain part of Canada, telling voters not to leave their fellow citizens behind.

Reaction among residents was mixed. Some residents who support remaining in Canada expressed frustration that the vote was called. Meanwhile, independence advocates dismissed recent agreements between Carney and Smith as insufficient assurances.
