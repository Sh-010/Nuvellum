Mexican filmmaker Ramón Llaven explores regional Day of the Dead customs in his documentary "Dance of the Souls," which screens at the Morelia Film Festival, DocsMX in Mexico City, and the Ji.hlava Documentary Film Festival. Produced by Fata Morgana and Eudiamonía Films alongside co-producers Estudios Churubusco Azteca and Lost Time Media, the film centers on the muerteadas of the Etla Valley in Oaxaca.

## A Returning Spirit's Perspective

The documentary utilizes a mystical narrative device following a wandering soul that returns during the Day of the Dead to visit familiar places and loved ones. Llaven, who spent roughly 15 years away from his native Oaxaca before returning to make the film, stated that his goal was to examine the local traditions from within. The project also incorporates childhood memories of a spiritual healer named Doña Julia and touches upon the untimely deaths of several young local men, including a friend of the director named Ricardo.

## Challenges and Production Hurdles

Production faced delays due to the pandemic, which required safety precautions and pushed the filming of the large-scale celebration from 2021 to 2023 and 2024. Additionally, Llaven noted that gaining the trust of the community and participants presented a significant challenge. The completed feature highlights the physical intensity of the muerteada, where participants wear elaborate costumes decorated with bells and mirrors that can weigh up to 20 kilograms while dancing alongside brass bands for hours.

## Reflecting Life and Death

While the exact origins of the Oaxacan muerteada remain debated between the Cañada and Mixteca regions, the celebration holds immense local significance, sometimes surpassing the importance of the new year or patron saint feast days. According to the director, the multi-hour carnival acts as a reflection of life, marking the end of one cycle and the beginning of another while welcoming death with joy.
