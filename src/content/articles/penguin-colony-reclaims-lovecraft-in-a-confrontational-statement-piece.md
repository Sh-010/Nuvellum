## Reinterpreting a classic horror framework

Developed by Origame Digital, the studio behind Umurangi Generation, *Penguin Colony* functions as a psychological horror game and a reinterpretation of H.P. Lovecraft’s 1936 novella *At The Mountains of Madness*. Set in Antarctica circa 1939, the roughly three-hour game follows a framing narrative voiced by Lenval Brown, incorporating elements from multiple Lovecraft stories alongside new artistic liberties.

In this version, two competing factions are drawn to Antarctica. Alongside a mysterious indigenous tribe, a Nazi expedition has deployed to the region in search of what they believe to be an Aryan God. The game's low-polygon visual style renders Antarctica as a dark, desolate expanse using aesthetics reminiscent of PlayStation 2 hardware.

## Gameplay mechanics and indigenous perspectives

Players control a lowly bird navigating the icy terrain through swimming, belly sliding, and hopping across craggy paths. Traversal puzzle elements allow players to swap between different penguin breeds, such as Rockhoppers for climbing and Emperors for sliding, though reviewer Giovanni Colantonio notes these mechanics are sparsely developed and accompanied by occasional bugs where characters clip through walls.

According to Origame Digital founder Naphtali Faulkner, who is a Māori game developer, the choice of protagonists centers on an indigenous framing of the setting. Faulkner stated in an interview that indigenous populations include animals as original inhabitants under colonization, viewing Antarctica not simply as no man's land but as a shared habitat threatened by European colonial powers.

## Confrontational messaging

Beyond its literary adaptation, the game incorporates explicit political messaging in its later stages. Through title cards, the developers criticize video game creators for insufficiently addressing contemporary crises, including Israel's war on Gaza, urging players to confront modern travesties with the same urgency applied to historical atrocities.

Reviewer Colantonio describes *Penguin Colony* as a confrontational work of protest art that weaponizes Lovecraftian cosmic horror against its author's well-documented ethnocentric worldview, recasting the narrative around themes of colonization and invasion.
