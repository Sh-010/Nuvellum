## Reflections on digital acting

Actor Andy Serkis has opened up about the technology behind his famous role as Gollum in "The Lord of the Rings" franchise, noting that public perception of performance capture remains widely misunderstood. Serkis, who is currently filming and directing "The Lord of the Rings: The Hunt for Gollum," stated that he previously felt a strong need to advocate for the medium.

He recalled previously spending hours discussing how the technology constitutes genuine acting, often watching listeners lose interest before eventually deciding to adopt a more relaxed attitude toward the debate.

## Comparing digital and physical masks

Drawing a parallel to traditional makeup effects, Serkis compared digital characters to historical physical transformations, such as John Hurt's appearance in "The Elephant Man." He noted that audiences seem more willing to accept a performance hidden beneath a physical prosthetic mask than one obscured by a digital counterpart.

Earlier in the year, filmmaker Peter Jackson addressed the ongoing challenges facing motion-capture roles, suggesting that anxieties surrounding artificial intelligence in cinema could prevent characters like Gollum from receiving industry awards. Jackson emphasized that Serkis' work remains entirely human-generated rather than computer-generated.

Serkis is set to reprise his role in the upcoming franchise installment, scheduled for theatrical release in December 2027, alongside a returning cast that includes Ian McKellen, Elijah Wood, and Lee Pace.
