## Airport struck for second consecutive day

King Khalid International Airport in the Saudi capital of Riyadh has been hit by a projectile strike, causing a fire in one of its passenger halls, according to officials. A Saudi military spokesman stated that air defences intercepted a missile near the airport on Sunday, while authorities worked to address the blaze. 

The civil aviation authority reported injuries from the incident. The strike occurred a day after a prior attack on the same facility resulted in 13 deaths and 308 injuries, following earlier strikes during the week. 

Yemen's Houthi rebels claimed responsibility for the attacks, issuing warnings to airlines and passengers against utilizing Saudi Arabian airports and airspace. The group stated it was targeting Saudi Arabia due to its support for government forces in Yemen's civil war, as well as an alleged air strike on the airport in Sanaa.

## Casualties and international response

Saturday's attack was the deadliest single strike on a Gulf Arab country since the war involving Israel, the US and Iran began in February. According to the Saudi civil aviation authority, the dead included four Saudi nationals, two people from Bangladesh, and one person each from the United States, Jordan, Palestine, Syria, Sudan, and Egypt. The Indian embassy in Saudi Arabia confirmed on Sunday that an Indian man had died from injuries sustained in the attack.

The Saudi-led coalition labelled Saturday's attack a "war crime" and vowed to respond. Meanwhile, Houthi-run Al Masirah TV reported that an air strike had been launched by "the Saudi enemy" on Sanaa International Airport, though Saudi Arabia has not confirmed the claim.

International leaders reacted to the escalation. US President Donald Trump stated that the United States would make a decision and move very quickly on whether to conduct strikes on the Houthis. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio condemned the attacks and confirmed the death of a US citizen. UK Defence Secretary Wes Streeting indicated the UK could potentially provide additional support to Saudi Arabia, while UK Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband expressed being "appalled" by the attack.

## Conflict escalation

Brig Gen Yahya Saree, the Houthi military spokesman, stated that the group had targeted the airport using a mix of ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, and drones over a 24-hour period. He vowed continued operations deep inside Saudi Arabia and designated Saudi airports as potential targets, excluding those in Mecca and Medina.

The recent violence follows the collapse in July of a temporary ceasefire brokered in 2022. The conflict intensified after Houthi forces seized stretches of the Red Sea coast, leading to Saudi strikes before pro-government forces secured the regions. The Houthis currently control approximately a quarter of Yemeni territory and about 80% of its population.
