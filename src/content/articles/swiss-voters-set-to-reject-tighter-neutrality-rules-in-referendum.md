## Referendum projection

Swiss voters appear to have overwhelmingly rejected a proposal to impose stricter constitutional limits on the country’s longstanding neutrality. Approximately 71 percent voted against the initiative in a nationwide referendum, based on a projection by the polling institute GFS Bern for the Swiss public broadcaster SRG. The projection indicated about 29 percent support for the measure.

The outcome preserves the government’s existing ability to impose sanctions and cooperate with NATO, rather than restricting those choices within a narrower constitutional definition. Switzerland has maintained international recognition as neutral since 1815, with neutrality forming part of its federal constitutional framework since 1848.

## Arguments for and against the initiative

The initiative was organised by Pro Switzerland, a sovereignty-focused group, and backed by the right-wing Swiss People’s Party (SVP), the country’s largest political party. Supporters contended that Switzerland's participation in European Union sanctions against Russia, as well as expanding security cooperation with NATO, had weakened its traditional neutral status.

Conversely, the Swiss government, parliament, and nearly all major political parties opposed the proposal. Opponents argued that the stricter limits would leave the country less equipped to respond to international crises.

## Context of the vote

Debates surrounding Swiss neutrality intensified following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. Switzerland's adoption of EU sanctions against Moscow drew criticism from Russia and from domestic proponents of stricter neutrality, who argued the government was taking sides.

Current policy permits Switzerland to cooperate internationally on security matters and participate in sanctions while remaining outside military alliances. The referendum focused on the degree of flexibility the government retains in applying neutrality amid shifting European security conditions.
