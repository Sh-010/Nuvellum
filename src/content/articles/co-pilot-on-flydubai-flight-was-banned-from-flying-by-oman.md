## Background on the flying ban

The man who allegedly stabbed his fellow pilot, the captain of an Israel-bound Flydubai flight, had reportedly been barred from flying in his native Oman over security concerns. According to a report by the Wall Street Journal, the co-pilot was hired by Flydubai despite the Omani ban, enabling him to operate a sensitive route between the United Arab Emirates and Israel.

Media reports and official statements offered varying details regarding the Omani restriction. US broadcaster CNN, citing two sources, reported that the co-pilot was removed from a position at Oman's national airline the previous year due to concerns over extremist views. Al Arabiya similarly cited an informed source stating that Omani authorities determined the co-pilot held extremist views. The Wall Street Journal noted that it could not be determined whether Oman shared its concerns regarding the pilot's radicalization with other countries or airlines, and the exact nature of the radicalization remained unclear.

## The flight incident and investigation

The attack occurred on a flight traveling from Dubai to Tel Aviv carrying 182 people on board, which included four pilots. During the flight, the co-pilot stabbed the captain in an apparent attempt to crash the aircraft. Two other pilots in the cabin intervened, took control of the plane, and landed safely in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu stated in a video release that authorities know the co-pilot underwent "Islamist radical indoctrination" with the intent to crash the jet. Netanyahu added that officials were investigating whether the co-pilot acted under the direction of others, warning that those involved would face a heavy price. The investigation into the incident remains ongoing.
