The 31st edition of the Busan International Film Festival (BIFF) drew its largest audience since 2015, recording 200,043 admissions for screenings. Total attendance, which included talks and outdoor events, reached 277,027. Running alongside the festival, the Asian Contents & Film Market (ACFM) hosted 30,484 visitors, marking a 1.6% increase from the previous year.

## Political controversy surrounds festival titles

The event was accompanied by political tensions concerning Hur Jin-ho’s thriller "The Assassin(s)," which centers on the 1974 shooting of first lady Yuk Young-soo. Despite topping the South Korean box office, the film drew criticism from conservative politicians and online groups over historical representation. Amid safety concerns and planned protests, production company Hive Media Corp. withdrew its cast and crew from festival events.

The controversy drew commentary from competition jury president Zhang Yimou, who noted that history is interpreted differently by individuals while emphasizing respect for creators' visions. Juror July Jung expressed regret over the friction surrounding the release. Later in the festival, a group of filmmakers held a press conference urging politicians to leave artistic evaluation to audiences, while criticizing opposition lawmakers who had written to the Singapore International Film Festival to request the film's withdrawal. Singapore retained the title as its opening film.

At the closing ceremony, Iranian director Majid Majidi won the Busan Award for best film with "Midday Meal." During his acceptance speech, Majidi criticized the war in Iran and referenced a U.S. military strike on a school in Minab.

## Producers form new alliances and address financing

During the ACFM, the Producers Guild of Korea and Indonesia's APROFI established the Asian Producers Alliance, with producer organizations from Taiwan, Hong Kong, and the Philippines also joining to coordinate co-productions and policy discussions. Financial agreements were also announced, including a KRW 100 billion ($74.6 million) content fund formed through an agreement between South Korea's Solaire Partners and Japan's Questry.

Industry participants noted ongoing challenges in independent film financing. ACFM director Ellen Y.D. Kim stated that public funding has not matched the rapid growth of the market, remaining level for two consecutive years.

## Artificial intelligence in production

The application of artificial intelligence was a central topic during panel discussions. Netflix senior director of APAC production Lee Sung Q reported that the platform has utilized AI in approximately 300 titles. Director Kang Yoon-sung estimated potential budget savings of 30% to 40% on features utilizing AI tools, though he noted that while technical output is high, current technology can fall short in delivering emotional resonance compared to human-driven production.
