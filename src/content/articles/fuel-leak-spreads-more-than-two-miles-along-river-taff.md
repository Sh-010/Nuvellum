Residents living near the River Taff have been advised to keep their windows and doors closed following a widespread fuel leak. According to Natural Resources Wales (NRW), kerosene has spread down nearly 2.5 miles (4 km) of the river.

The incident was initially reported on Saturday morning as affecting at least one mile of the waterway. NRW stated that it continues to investigate the source of the incident to understand how it occurred, halt the pollution, and collect evidence. Officials noted that the pollution was discharging from an underground surface water drainage system, though the amount of fuel has since reduced significantly.

## Impact on the local community

Locals in Taffs Well, Rhondda Cynon Taff, reported strong fumes beginning on Friday. Eurig Thomas described the area as smelling "like a petrol station" and noted a shiny sheen on the stream flowing behind his home from a nearby GE factory. Another resident, Sarah Llewelyn, stated that the fumes were strong enough to cause eye irritation and that South Wales Fire and Rescue had visited the site on Friday to stop the flow.

Public Health Wales advised individuals living nearby to avoid any contact with the water, keep windows and doors shut, and contact NHS 111 if they felt unwell.

## Response and containment efforts

Absorbent materials and equipment have been deployed by NRW and the fire service to collect the fuel and contain the leak. Water samples have also been collected to assess the environmental damage caused by the pollution. 

Gareth Llewelyn, a former hazardous materials environmental protection officer with the South Wales Fire and Rescue service, expressed concern over the long-term environmental impact, stating that the situation requires extensive restoration efforts rather than a quick fix.
