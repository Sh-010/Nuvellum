## Lawsuits filed over Minneapolis shooting

The family of Renee Good, a 37-year-old U.S. citizen who was fatally shot in her car by a U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agent in Minneapolis on January 7, has filed civil lawsuits against the administration of U.S. President Donald Trump.

Good and her wife had participated in daily protests in Minneapolis opposing the deployment of armed federal agents sent to Minnesota to identify and arrest undocumented migrants for deportation under an initiative known as Operation Metro Surge.

Two civil suits were filed on October 1 by the Romanucci Blandin Law firm. One names the United States of America as the sole defendant, while the second names various government and border control officials. The individual defendants include the ICE officer who shot Good, White House Deputy Chief of Staff for Policy Stephen Miller, and the individual serving as U.S. Secretary of Homeland Security at the time of the shooting, Kristi Noem.

The plaintiffs are Good's brother Brent Ganger, acting as trustee for her next of kin including her three children, and her partner Becca Good.

## Allegations of unconstitutional force

The lawsuits allege negligence, civil rights violations, federal conspiracy with discriminatory design, and the authorized use of force against observers. The legal filings claim that extreme and unconstitutional force was exerted against individuals exercising their rights under the U.S. Constitution's Bill of Rights.

A statement from the Romanucci Blandin Law firm asserted that the killing was the predictable result of a coordinated campaign by senior administration officials targeting Minnesotans of Somali or Hispanic origin and silencing protesters.

The legal complaints further allege that Operation Metro Surge relied on exaggerated fraud accusations that served as a pretext for masked federal agents to patrol neighborhoods and stop individuals based on appearance. According to the firm, officials instructed agents to force confrontations with observers like Good and fostered a belief that they would face no consequences for using force.

## Background of Operation Metro Surge

Good's death, along with the fatal shooting two weeks later of nurse Alex Pretti, intensified existing protests against the deployment of ICE and Customs and Border Protection agents in Minneapolis. The deployment occurred amid President Trump's strict stance on migration and border security, and assertions that local authorities in Democrat-run states inadequately addressed crime and irregular migration.

Following the protests and the deaths of Good and Pretti, Operation Metro Surge experienced a winding-down period and ceased entirely by mid-February.
