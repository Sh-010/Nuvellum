Actor Sylvester Stallone has opened up about his tumultuous relationship with his body and the physical transformations he underwent for his film roles, including the 'Rocky' franchise. 

## Body transformations and obsession

In an interview with The New York Times, Stallone discussed how his approach to physical fitness changed across different projects. He described designing his physique for different characters, aiming for a smooth look for Rocky and a more feral, athletic appearance for Rambo. 

However, Stallone admitted that this mindset escalated into an obsession. He stated that he became bulimic while preparing for 'Rocky III,' reaching 2.6 percent body fat and struggling to keep food down due to his fixation on maintaining a specific level of fitness.

## The toll of steroid use

Stallone also addressed the use of performance-enhancing drugs during his career, stating that maintaining such fitness levels for months at a time required additives and drugs. He described using substances in cycles, noting that he used them "in a sensitive way — six weeks on, six weeks off." 

While acknowledging that achieving such physiques is not possible naturally, Stallone emphasized that the process carried a heavy cost. 

## Finding liberation

Stallone noted that playing a weaker, overweight, and partially deaf character in the 1997 film 'Cop Land' offered a sense of liberation from his previous routines. Reflecting on his career, he stated that treating his body in such a masochistic manner came at a terrible price and expressed regret over performing his own stunts.

Stallone's forthcoming memoir, "The Steps," is scheduled for release on Sept. 29.
